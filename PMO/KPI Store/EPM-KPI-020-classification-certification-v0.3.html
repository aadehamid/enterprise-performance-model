01Why this exists

The inventory is complete. Nothing in it has governance status. This framework is how the inventory becomes a catalog.

Every extracted calculation currently sits at Tier 8 — unvalidated report logic — regardless of how widely it was used or how confidently it was named. The job is to sort it, promote a subset, and give the rest a home that is not the bin. Three things make that hard, and this document exists to handle each:

  • The word "KPI" was applied by whoever built the report. A calculation named "Throughput KPI" is evidence that someone called it that, nothing more.
  • Classification is a judgement, and judgements need stated criteria or they become arguments about who is more senior.
  • Reviewing is not approving. Two validation steps are planned; neither creates an accountable owner. Only a governance act does.
The one-sentence purpose

Turn "here are all the calculations we found" into a defensible register where every item has a classification, a disposition, a rationale, and a named next step.

Say this early and often: classification is not deletion

Most calculations will not become KPIs. Almost all of them still get a home — the Enterprise Measurement Catalog, a local report scope, or a documented retirement. "Not a KPI" is a routing decision, not a verdict on someone's work. Teams that hear the second thing rather than the first stop cooperating around session three, and the review never recovers.

02Fixing the taxonomy before using it

The Charter's twelve classes are the right content in the wrong shape. Used as written, they produce classification arguments that are really axis confusion.

The problem

The twelve classes mix three independent questions into one list:

Charter classIs actually a…
Raw measure · Business measure · Derived measure · Operational metric · Diagnostic metric · Performance indicator · Technical calculation · Presentation-only calculationKind — what sort of thing it is
Candidate KPI · Approved enterprise KPI · Obsolete or retiredGovernance state — where it sits on the ladder
Duplicate or near-duplicateDisposition — what we are going to do with it

Used as a single list, this forces false choices. Is a duplicate of an approved KPI a "duplicate" or an "approved KPI"? Both — on different axes. Is a candidate KPI still a performance indicator? Yes. The list cannot express that, so people argue.

The fix — three independent axes

AxisQuestionValues
KindWhat sort of thing is this?Presentation-only · Technical calculation · Raw measure · Business measure · Derived measure · Operational metric · Diagnostic metric · Performance indicator · KPI
DispositionWhat do we do with it?Promote · Consolidate into another · Retain locally · Reference only · Retire
Governance stateWhere is it on the ladder?Extracted · Classified · Candidate · Approved · Implemented · Superseded · Retired
Why this is worth the amendment

Every item gets three values instead of one, and each is a separate, answerable question. "This is a derived measure, disposition consolidate into MEAS-114, state classified" is unambiguous and reviewable. "This is a duplicate" is not. The Charter's content is preserved entirely — only the structure changes. Decision DC-01.

These three axes are an inventory taxonomy for extracted calculations. They are not the Constraints four property axes (authority, hardness, horizon, constrained object), and they are not a meaning taxonomy. Meaning is authored in the ontology and signed at G3/G4 CV/CQ. Batching by business area is for review stamina only — it is not a domain boundary.

The one-line explanation Kind is what it is. Disposition is what happens to it. State is how far through governance it has travelled. Nothing is lost by separating them, and the arguments mostly disappear.

03Normalise before classifying

Classifying an un-normalised inventory means classifying the same calculation eleven times and getting inconsistent answers. Normalisation comes first, and its hardest problem is near-duplicates.

Normalisation steps

#StepMethod
N-1Syntactic normalisationStrip whitespace, standardise casing, resolve aliases, normalise operator ordering and parenthesisation. Catches trivially identical formulas written by different hands
N-2Source-field resolutionResolve every referenced field to its physical source. Two formulas that look different may reference the same columns; two that look identical may not
N-3Semantic equivalence groupingDifferent expressions producing the same result — a/b*100 and 100*(a/b), or a sum-of-differences versus a difference-of-sums
N-4Name clusteringGroup by name similarity as a hint only — never as evidence. See the rule below
N-5Result comparisonWhere data is available, execute candidates over the same period and compare. The only definitive test
N-6Variant recordingWhere candidates are not equivalent, record precisely how they differ. This difference is usually the most valuable finding in the whole exercise
The near-duplicate rule

Never merge on name similarity alone. Two calculations both called "Refinery Throughput" that differ by one excluded product code, one date-boundary convention, or one unit basis are not duplicates — they are two different measures with the same name, and merging them destroys a real business distinction that someone deliberately created.

Merge only on formula equivalence plus source equivalence plus filter equivalence. Where two candidates differ, both survive as separate items and the difference is logged as a definitional conflict for the SME review. Decision DC-03.

The finding hiding in the variants

Near-duplicates that differ subtly are the strongest evidence you will find of the definitional drift the whole initiative exists to fix. Six variants of "loss" across four reports is not a data quality problem — it is six teams having six different conversations about the same word, and it is exactly what the Store catalog is meant to resolve. Surface these deliberately in the SME review rather than quietly picking one.

04The classification decision tree

Ask the cheapest and most eliminating questions first. Most of the inventory exits in the first three steps, which is what makes the exercise tractable.

1
Is it a presentation artifact?Colour rules, labels, sort keys, tooltip text, formatting strings, display flags. Produces appearance, not a business number.
Yes → Presentation-only. Retire. But see the extraction rule below.
2
Is it a technical helper?Row indexes, join keys, parameter placeholders, filter flags, date scaffolding, sequence numbers. Exists to make the report work.
Yes → Technical calculation. Retire.
3
Is it equivalent to something already classified?Formula, source, and filters all equivalent — per §03.
Yes → Disposition: consolidate. Record the target.
4
Does it produce a number a business person would recognise as meaning something?If you cannot state what it means in one business sentence, it does not pass.
No → Technical calculation. Retire.
5
Does it come directly from source with no transformation?A count, a sum, a value read as-is.
Yes → Raw measure. Catalog it.
6
Is it a straightforward business aggregate of raw measures?Total volume sold. Number of deliveries.
Yes → Business measure. Catalog it.
7
Is it computed from other measures?Ratios, rates, variances, indices.
Yes → Derived measure. Catalog it, then continue.
8
Is it watched over time, in context, by someone?Not "could be" — is it actually monitored?
Yes → Metric. Operational if it tracks running the business; diagnostic if it explains why something moved.
9
Does it indicate performance against an expectation?There is a notion of better and worse, not just higher and lower.
Yes → Performance indicator. Continue to the qualification test.
10
Does it pass the full KPI qualification test in §06?All eleven criteria, evidenced.
Yes → Candidate KPI.
No → Performance indicator; record which criteria failed.
The extraction rule for step 1

Presentation-only calculations frequently encode real business logic that exists nowhere else — most commonly a conditional colour rule that hard-codes a threshold. IF value < 0.97 THEN red is a governed threshold wearing a formatting costume.

Before retiring any presentation calculation, extract any embedded threshold, target, or business rule and classify that separately. The formatting is presentation; the number inside it may be the only surviving record of an agreed performance expectation. Risk RK-10.

Steps 5–7 are not exits A calculation can be a derived measure and a performance indicator and a candidate KPI. The measure classification says what it is built from; the later steps say what role it plays. Record the measure class and keep going — the Enterprise Measurement Catalog needs the measure regardless of whether the KPI survives.

The distinction most often collapsed

Performance indicator versus KPI. Most of what an organisation calls its KPIs are performance indicators — real, meaningful, watched, and missing an accountable owner or a defined action. That is not a failing; it is the normal state of an ungoverned estate. The value of separating them is that the gap becomes visible and fixable: "this is a performance indicator that needs an owner and a threshold to become a KPI" is a specific, actionable finding.

06The KPI qualification test

The Charter's eleven criteria, made checkable. Each needs a yes and evidence. Crucially, they split into criteria we can assess and criteria only the business can supply — and that split determines what each review step is for.

#CriterionEvidence requiredAssessed by
K-1Defined business objectiveA named objective the KPI serves, not a restatement of the KPIBusiness
K-2Accountable business ownerA named individual who has accepted accountability. Not a departmentBusiness
K-3Approved meaning, then certified formulaBusiness wording is input to CV/CQ — not the SoT. Ontology authors meaning; G3/G4 signed CV/CQ is the meaning SoT. Formula is authored by the formula author in the Store catalog + consumption semantic layerOntology + formula author
K-4Approved calculation, in executable formThe full field set from KPI-000 §05 — expression, bindings, aggregation, additivity, grainUs
K-5Defined dimensions and time grainLegal slicing dimensions and atomic grain, declaredUs
K-6Target or performance expectationA value, with an owner and an effective periodBusiness
K-7Thresholds or evaluation logicKPI-owner Thresholds (RAG) referencing a LimitValue. Constraint ≠ Threshold ≠ Breach ≠ Binding. Constraint authority = physics / permit / spec / SOL — not the KPI ownerKPI owner
K-8Management review cadenceA named forum and frequency that actually meetsBusiness
K-9Defined action when it movesA stated decision or intervention. See the test belowBusiness
K-10Enterprise or cross-report significanceUsed beyond one report or one team, or explicitly enterprise-designatedUs
K-11Feasible data quality and timelinessLineage complete to source; refresh meets the cadence in K-8Us
K-9 is the test that does the real work

If nobody would do anything differently when this number changes, it is not a KPI.

Ask it exactly that way, of a specific person, about a specific number: "It moves from 94 to 89. What happens? Who does what?" A KPI produces a concrete answer — a review is triggered, a plan is changed, someone is called. A performance indicator produces "well, we'd look into it." That difference is the whole distinction, and it is the single most efficient question in this framework.

Four we assess

K-4, K-5, K-10, K-11

Executable definition, dimensions and grain, cross-report significance, data feasibility. All determinable from the inventory, the lineage, and the platform. These should be complete before the SME review, so the business is never asked to rule on something we could have established ourselves.

Six only the business can supply

K-1, K-2, K-6, K-7, K-8, K-9

Objective, owner, target, Threshold, cadence, action. The owner owns those. Meaning (K-3) is authored in the ontology and signed at G3/G4 CV/CQ — the owner's wording is input, not the SoT. Store client validation (KPI-020) is not domain CV/CQ.

Partial passes are the normal outcome

Most performance indicators will pass K-1, K-3, K-4, K-5, K-10, K-11 and fail on owner, target, threshold, cadence, or action. That is not a rejection — it is a gap list, and it is the most useful thing this framework produces. "This becomes a KPI when someone owns it and states what happens when it breaches" is a specific ask that a sponsor can act on.

Rung 7 is a different kind of event from rungs 1–6

Rungs one to six are analysis and agreement. Rung seven is certification — one name, one population, one formula, one grain, versioned, consumption-SL-only compile, Gold snapshot — plus a named owner accepting accountability for target, Threshold, cadence, and action. The owner act is necessary, not sufficient. Meaning was already signed at G3/G4 CV/CQ; the owner does not author it. Every KPI Store that ends up as an unused catalog got to rung six and stopped, because rung seven requires both the compile path and a conversation about accountability.

08SME review protocol

The in-house review. Its job is to confirm the classification and check that definitions are faithful to what the business means — in that order.

What it is and is not

It is

A classification review, then a definition review

"Do you agree this is a diagnostic metric and not a KPI?" is a fast question with a decisive answer. Asking it first removes most of the inventory from the slower conversation about exact wording.

It is not

An approval, or a renegotiation of the taxonomy

SMEs challenge individual classifications with evidence. They do not redefine what a KPI is mid-review — if the taxonomy is wrong, that escalates as a standards question and the review pauses.

Running it

PracticeDetail
Batch by business areaFor review stamina only — not a meaning taxonomy and not a domain boundary. One area's SMEs can move fast through their own inventory and cannot usefully rule on another's
Pre-send the batchClassification plus one-line rationale per item, at least three days ahead. Reviewing cold in the room is where stamina goes
Timebox per itemTwo to three minutes for confirmations, five for disputes. Anything longer is logged and taken offline — one contested item can consume a session
Lead with the variantsOpen with the near-duplicate groups from §03. These are the most interesting items and they warm the room up on real content rather than on admin
Capture disagreement as evidence, not as instant reclassificationAn SME saying "that's definitely a KPI" is input. Reclassify after the session, against the criteria, and show what changed
Record every changed classificationThe list of what the review changed is the evidence the review was genuine rather than a rubber stamp
Cap the sessionRoughly ninety minutes, or attention collapses and later items get waved through. Volume is the enemy — plan more short sessions, not fewer long ones
The framing that makes the session productive

"We are not asking whether these calculations are good. We are asking what kind of thing each one is, so that the ones that need enterprise governance get it and the ones that do not are left alone." That framing lowers the stakes on every individual item and makes disagreement cheap — which is what you want, because the disagreements are the useful part.

09Store client validation (KPI-020)

A different conversation from the SME review, and a different conversation from domain CV/CQ. This one is about ownership and consequence — target, Threshold, cadence, action. Meaning is not decided here.

Store client validation ≠ CV/CQ

Call this Store client validation (KPI-020). Domain controlled vocabulary and competency questions are signed at G3/G4. Do not certify a KPI whose terms are not in a signed CV. An “agreed business definition in the business’s own words” is input to CV/CQ, not the meaning SoT. The ontology authors meaning.

What Store client validation must produce, per KPI

  1. Agreement that this is a Store KPI rather than a Measurement Catalog metric or a process metric
  2. Confirmation that the terms already sit in a signed CV — or a block until they do
  3. A named individual accepting accountability for target, Threshold, cadence, and action
  4. A target, with an effective period
  5. Thresholds with severity and direction (KPI-owner authority; Constraint ≠ Threshold)
  6. A review forum and cadence that actually exists
  7. A stated action when performance moves
  8. customer_grain ∈ {payer, sold-to, site} when the KPI is a Store pilot. Name Customer Account + Sold-To / Bill-To / Payer / Ship-To (location) / CreditAccount (on payer). Do not overload as “Customer”
Validation is not approval

A room agreeing that a formula looks correct produces items 1 and 2. It does not produce items 3 to 7, and without those the Charter's own criteria are unmet. Certification requires a named person to accept accountability for target, Threshold, cadence, and action — and the compile path, grain, and Gold snapshot. That is a harder and often separate conversation, and it should be scheduled as such rather than assumed to happen inside a validation workshop.

Running it

PracticeWhy
Bring candidates with gap lists, not blank forms"Here is the proposed definition, and it needs an owner and a threshold" is answerable. "Tell us about your KPIs" is a two-hour discussion producing nothing recordable
Ask K-9 of a named person, about a specific number"It drops to 89 — what happens?" Concrete answer or it is not a KPI. This question resolves more classifications than any other
Separate the owner conversationAccepting accountability in front of peers is a real commitment. Some people need to consider it, and pressing in the room produces a nod rather than an owner
Present the variants for resolutionWhere six definitions of "loss" exist, this is the forum that picks one — or decides that three are genuinely different and names them differently
Record everything that stays a gapA candidate with three unmet criteria and named owners for closing each is a better outcome than a forced approval
Do not approve on the day by defaultValidation produces the material for approval. Approval is a separate, recorded governance act — see rung 7

10Awkward cases

Six situations that will arise, with a position decided in advance — because deciding them in the moment favours whoever is most insistent.

A widely used calculation fails the KPI test Certain

Position. Usage is consumption evidence, not governance evidence. It classifies as a metric or performance indicator, goes into the Enterprise Measurement Catalog, and remains available to every report that uses it. Nothing breaks.

Position to state. "This stays exactly where it is and keeps working. It is not going in the KPI Store because it has no owner and no defined action — and if you want it there, those are the two things to add."

What not to do. Promote it to keep the peace. One unqualified KPI in the catalog licenses the next twenty.

Two near-identical calculations, two owners, both insist theirs is correct Likely

Position. Do not merge and do not arbitrate on the spot. Both survive as separate candidates; the difference is logged as a definitional conflict with the precise divergence stated (which filter, which date rule, which unit basis).

Escalation. Cross-domain definitional conflicts go to the Global Process Owner or the governance forum, not to the modeling team. Our job is to make the difference precise, not to decide whose business meaning wins.

Frequent outcome. Both are right for their own context and the resolution is two differently-named KPIs, not one merged one. That is a legitimate and often correct answer.

Everyone wants it, but the data cannot support it Likely

Position. It stays a Candidate with a K-11 feasibility blocker recorded. Do not approve something that cannot be computed reliably — a certified KPI that silently produces wrong numbers damages the store more than a missing one.

Useful reframing. The blocker becomes a data-quality or sourcing requirement with a named owner. "This is approved the day we can source X reliably" is a productive outcome and often motivates the sourcing work.

No one will own it Certain, for some

Position. K-2 is not waivable. Without a named accountable owner it cannot be a KPI — by definition, not by policy. It classifies as a performance indicator with an ownership gap.

What this reveals. An unownable KPI usually means one of three things: nobody actually uses it for decisions, it spans an organisational boundary nobody wants to cross, or it measures something people would rather not be accountable for. All three are findings worth reporting to the sponsor.

Do not. Assign a department as owner. "Commercial owns it" means nobody owns it.

An executive's favourite number fails the test The hardest one

Position. Classify it honestly against the criteria and show the specific gaps. Then let the sponsor decide — an override is legitimate, and it must be recorded as an override with its rationale rather than absorbed by quietly relaxing the criteria.

Why recording matters more than the outcome. One recorded override is a decision. Three unrecorded ones mean the criteria no longer apply to anyone, and every subsequent classification is negotiable. Decision DC-07.

Often the gaps are closable. An executive who cares about a number will usually supply an owner and a threshold when asked directly. The test frequently converts these rather than rejecting them.

Unbilled — a STATE, not a KPI and not a customer class Awkward kind

Position. Unbilled is a STATE of an account, invoice, or movement (unbilled versus billed). It is not a KPI, not a customer class, and not a reason to force a demo metric up the measure → KPI ladder.

What to do with a demo that counts unbilled items. Keep it as a measure or operational metric in the Measurement Catalog. If a Store-certified KPI later needs that state, it references the state and a domain IRI — it does not promote “Unbilled” itself.

What not to do. Treat Unbilled as a customer class, or certify it because the demo is visible. Do not rebuild the Unbilled demo to make this point.

The calculation is Power BI-local, created after migration Depends on extraction scope

Position. Same classification treatment, but record the origin separately. Post-migration local measures are a distinct population — they are more recent, more likely to be actively used, and more likely to duplicate something that already exists centrally.

Why the distinction is worth keeping. The rate at which report authors create local measures after a governed catalog exists is a direct measure of whether the catalog is working. Track it.

Open question QK-12 asks whether the extraction covered this population at all.

11The funnel

Expectation management. Set this shape with the client before results land — after, it reads as an excuse. Tier 9 — indicative shape, not a prediction about this estate.

Candidate KPIs — passing the assessable criteria

Approved enterprise KPIs — with owner, target, threshold, cadence, action

Why deliberately no percentages

Published ratios would be invented, and this estate's shape depends entirely on how the Tableau content was built — a workbook-heavy estate with many local variants funnels very differently from a small set of governed dashboards. Report the shape now and the numbers after normalisation, when they are measured rather than guessed.

The one thing worth saying confidently: an organisation's approved enterprise KPI count almost always lands in the tens. A catalog of four hundred approved KPIs is not a governed catalog; it is the original problem with a new name.

The reassurance that makes the funnel acceptable Everything that leaves the KPI track goes somewhere. Measures and metrics populate the Enterprise Measurement Catalog and stay available to every report. Only presentation and technical calculations are genuinely retired, and those were never business content. Nobody loses a number they were using.

The three that decide whether the catalog is trusted

RK-03 (over-promotion), RK-06 (validation mistaken for approval), and RK-07 (no owners). Each is prevented by holding a single line that will be uncomfortable at least once.

DC-07OverridesPermitted by the sponsor, recorded as overrides with rationale, and reported in the funnel report. Never absorbed by relaxing criteria
DC-08Destination for non-promoted itemsThe Enterprise Measurement Catalog, not the bin. Only presentation and technical calculations are genuinely retired
DC-09Presentation-calculation extractionMine every conditional formatting rule for embedded thresholds before retiring it
DC-10Approval as a distinct actRung 7 is a recorded governance act by a named owner, scheduled separately from validation

15Glossary

TermWhat it actually means
ClassificationDeciding what kind of thing a calculation is. Three separate questions: what it is, what happens to it, how far through governance it has travelled
KindWhat sort of thing it is — presentation, technical, measure, metric, indicator, or KPI
DispositionWhat we are going to do with it — promote, consolidate, retain locally, reference, or retire
Governance stateWhere it sits on the ladder: extracted · classified · candidate · approved · implemented · superseded · retired
NormalisationRecognising that eleven differently-written calculations are the same calculation — before deciding anything about them
Near-duplicateTwo calculations that look the same and are not. The subtle difference is usually deliberate and usually important
Equivalence groupA set of calculations proven to produce the same result, represented by one member
Presentation-onlyProduces appearance, not a number. Retire it — but mine it first for hard-coded thresholds
Technical calculationExists to make the report work. A business person would never ask for its value
MeasureA number with agreed meaning
MetricA measure watched over time in context
Performance indicatorA metric with a notion of good and bad, but missing an owner, a target, or a defined action. Most of what organisations call KPIs
KPIA performance indicator that can meet certification. If nobody would do anything differently when it moves, it is not one. Meaning is not an owner decision
Candidate KPIPasses what we can assess, awaiting the business criteria and a governance act
CertificationOne name, one population, one formula, one grain, versioned, consumption-SL-only compile, Gold snapshot. Owner act is necessary, not sufficient. Terms must already sit in a signed CV
Store client validation (KPI-020)Owner, target, Threshold, cadence, action. Not domain CV/CQ. Not approval
UnbilledA STATE of an account, invoice, or movement. Not a KPI. Not a customer class. Demo metrics that count it stay measures
Store catalogThe certified catalog. Not the ontology. Not the Measurement Catalog. Figure and process KPIs stay out of it
Tier elevationMoving a calculation from unvalidated report logic up to approved enterprise KPI through defined steps, each of which adds evidence
OverrideA sponsor decision to promote something that fails a criterion. Legitimate, and only safe when recorded as an override rather than absorbed
Definitional conflictTwo variants of the same-named thing that genuinely differ. Documented rather than resolved by the modeling team — and one of the most valuable outputs of the exercise
