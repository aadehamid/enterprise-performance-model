The sequencing correction this plan makes

Process mapping and lineage are running ahead of classification. That is not wrong — both are needed for candidates — but it means effort is currently being spent contextualising calculations that classification will retire. Bring classification forward to run alongside, and prioritise contextualisation on items that survive the first classification pass. Otherwise a meaningful share of the lineage work is mapped and then discarded.

The difference in workshop character from Workstream 2

Domain Modeling workshops are few and deep — three-hour modeling sessions with the same people. KPI Store inventory classification is many and shallow — ninety-minute review batches with rotating business-area SMEs, driven by inventory volume. That discipline is for classification only, not for Store pilot delivery. Different fatigue profile, different scheduling discipline. Plan more short sessions for classification; do not apply that pattern to certification.

Three sequencing rules that protect this schedule

Taxonomy gates classification. K1 cannot start before GK0 (week 4, after the week-3 gate). Classifying against a draft taxonomy means reclassifying later.
Classification gates contextualisation effort. Contextualise survivors, not everything.
Certified formulas gate the compile path. Databricks Metric Views cannot be pointed at prose — the K-4 criterion must be met at candidate stage, not at build time.
Hamid gates publish. K4.8 warehouse / Purview publish does not start until Hamid opens a slice (DP-18).

Sizing — planning envelope, restated

The cartesian product of the high column (8 areas × 3 SME + 8 × 2 validation + 10 owners + 4 threshold + 9 fixed) is 63 and is not the booking target. The 26–42 range is the planning envelope.

Realistic centre 30–34. Publish the resolved number after CK1. Do not commit to a session count before the inventory is normalised — the count depends on equivalence groups, not on raw extracted items. The scheduling constraint that will bite

Thirty-plus sessions across four to eight business areas, over roughly twelve weeks of K3, with rotating SMEs. That is two to three client sessions per week sustained for a quarter. It needs a dedicated facilitator and a scheduler, and the whole series must be booked at CK2 — not arranged week by week. A review series that slips into ad hoc scheduling loses continuity, and continuity is what keeps classifications consistent across batches.

05CK1 — baseline confirmation and scope

Not a cold kickoff. Work is underway, so CK1's job is to confirm the baseline, set the funnel expectation before results land, and resolve the sizing variables.

What CK1 must produce

  1. Confirmation of where the work actually stands, and agreement on the sequencing correction
  2. The funnel expectation, set before any classification results exist
  3. Agreement to ratify the three-axis taxonomy, and a route to do so
  4. Understanding and acceptance of the dual-path architecture and what it obligates
  5. Identification of who can accept KPI accountability, by business area
  6. A written acceptance test for "demonstrate ad hoc computation"
  7. Values for the five sizing variables, so the calendar can be published
The single most important thing to achieve at CK1

Set the funnel expectation. The client currently believes they have N KPIs because N calculations were extracted. Explaining afterwards why that number collapsed reads as an excuse. Explaining beforehand — with the tier-elevation frame and the reassurance that classification is not deletion — reads as expertise. This is a fifteen-minute conversation that determines the political temperature of the next six months.

CK1 questions

Baseline and scope
  1. How many calculations were extracted, and how many does the business believe are KPIs?
  2. Did extraction capture conditional formatting and colour rules? Those often hold the only record of an agreed threshold.
  3. Does the inventory include Power BI measures created after migration, or only Tableau-derived logic?
  4. Has any normalisation been done, or is the count raw items?
  5. Is there an existing KPI list the business already treats as authoritative?
Governance and ownership
  1. Who can accept accountability for a KPI? Ask per business area, and ask for names.
  2. Is there a governance forum that can perform a formal approval act, or must one be created?
  3. Does EPM-MEAS-001 exist, and who ratifies an amendment to it?
  4. What happens today when two reports disagree on the same number? Who resolves it?
  5. Are there KPIs with regulatory or external reporting obligations? Those have different approval requirements.
The demonstration and the architecture
  1. What does "demonstrate ad hoc computation" mean as an acceptance test? Which questions, asked by whom, against what data, with what response time?
  2. Is the AI agent being built by this project, another team, or not yet?
  3. Closed: programme compiler is Databricks Metric Views (Hamid, 2026-08-14), until Hamid reopens again. MetricFlow is the demo-1 compiler only. Is Ossie in as interchange only?
  4. Has the Silver metadata table been built or designed yet? Is it intended to be editable?
  5. What refresh cadence do the Gold values need, and what latency will leadership accept?
  6. What restatement history does leadership reporting require — can last quarter's pack be reproduced exactly?
  7. Does row-level security apply to KPI consumption?
Logistics
  1. How many business areas should the review batches follow?
  2. Who are the SMEs per area, and what is their realistic availability across a quarter?
  3. Can we get execution access to run calculations for equivalence comparison?
  4. Are workshops in person, remote, or mixed?
  5. What else is competing for the same governance people — including our own Workstream 2?

06CK2 onward

CK2 Ownership identification Half dayK0

Purpose. Convert CK1's nominations into a working list, and book the entire review series before diaries fill.

Attendees. Sponsor, governance lead, business-area leads, prospective owners.

Note on framing. This session identifies owners. It does not secure acceptance — that happens in individual sessions during K3.6, because accepting accountability in front of peers produces a nod rather than a commitment.

Critical output. The full K3 session calendar, booked. Thirty sessions arranged week by week will not happen.

CK3 Taxonomy and criteria ratification Half dayK0

Purpose. Ratify the three-axis taxonomy and the eleven KPI qualification criteria as the published standard. Everything in K1 and K3 is measured against this.

Attendees. Enterprise Data Governance, sponsor, business-area representatives, our lead analyst.

Method. Apply the criteria live to ten real calculations from the inventory — a mix of obvious KPIs, obvious non-KPIs, and genuinely marginal ones. The marginal cases are where the criteria get tested.

Why this must precede K1. Classification against an unratified taxonomy is reclassification waiting to happen, and it hands anyone who dislikes a result a procedural objection.

CK4–CKn SME classification review series 90 min eachK3

Purpose. Confirm or correct classifications, batched by business area.

Discipline. Pre-send three days ahead. Two to three minutes per confirmation, five per dispute, anything longer taken offline. Ninety-minute cap — attention collapses after that and later items get waved through, which is how inconsistency enters the record.

Open with the variants. The near-duplicate groups are the most interesting content and they warm the room on real substance rather than admin.

Record every changed classification. That list is the evidence the review was genuine rather than a rubber stamp — and it is what you show when someone later asks whether the SMEs were actually listened to.

CK-DC Definitional conflict workshop Full dayK3

Purpose. Resolve — or formally split — the cases where the same name covers genuinely different calculations. This is the workshop that most directly addresses why the initiative exists.

Attendees. All affected business areas together. This is deliberately cross-functional; running it per-area defeats the point.

Method. Per conflict: present the variants, state the precise difference, ask each area what theirs means and why. Then either converge on one definition, or agree they are two things and name them differently.

Legitimate outcome. Splitting is often correct. Two differently-named KPIs is a better result than one merged definition that satisfies nobody and quietly breaks two reports.

Escalation. Unresolvable conflicts go to the Global Process Owner or the governance forum. Our job is to make the difference precise, not to arbitrate whose business meaning wins.

CK-V Store client validation (KPI-020) 2 hours eachK3

Purpose. Owner, target, Threshold, cadence, action. Not domain CV/CQ. Do not certify a KPI whose terms are not in a signed CV. Meaning is authored in the ontology.

Method. Bring candidates with gap lists, never blank forms. "Here is the proposed definition; it needs an owner and a threshold" is answerable in minutes. "Tell us about your KPIs" consumes two hours and produces nothing recordable.

Ask K-9 directly, of a named person, about a specific number. "It drops to 89 — what happens? Who does what?" This single question resolves more classifications than everything else combined.

Do not approve on the day. Validation produces the material for approval. Approval is a separate recorded act.

CK-O Ownership acceptance sessions 30–45 min eachK3

Purpose. Secure named individuals accepting accountability. Held individually or in very small groups.

Why separate. Accepting accountability is a real commitment with real consequences. Asked in a large workshop, people nod. Asked individually, with the obligations stated plainly, people either commit or decline — and a clear decline is far more useful than a soft yes that evaporates at approval.

State the obligations. Approving the definition, setting and owning the target, attending the review cadence, and taking the defined action. If someone will not do those four things, they are not the owner.

Report declines as findings. An unownable KPI usually means it is not used for decisions, spans a boundary nobody wants to cross, or measures something people would rather not answer for. All three are worth telling the sponsor.

CK-D Ad hoc computation demonstration Half dayK5

Purpose. Prove that a certified formula compiles via Databricks Metric Views to SQL (programme pilots on a Databricks SQL warehouse in the Azure account) and returns a correct answer to a question nobody precomputed — against the acceptance test agreed at K0.9. Same compile must match the Gold snapshot (RC-2). The existing demo pack remains MetricFlow. A learning / demo-2 workspace, if shown, is Databricks Free edition only — not Azure Databricks, not Synapse, not Fabric.

Attendees. Sponsor, business-area leads, KPI owners, data platform lead, AI initiative lead if one exists.

Structure. Show a certified Gold value. Then ask a question Gold cannot answer — a different slice, a different period, a combination nobody built. Show the compiled SQL. Show the result. Then show the reconciliation: ask the ad hoc path the same question Gold answers, and show the numbers match.

The part not to skip. Demonstrate the refusal. Ask for a non-additive measure aggregated illegally and show the compiler declining rather than answering wrongly. Audiences remember the guardrail more than the capability, and it is the thing that earns trust in the certified path.

Be precise about what has been proven. A demonstration on pilot KPIs is not a production capability across the catalog. Say so in the room.

CK-R Reconciliation review Half dayK5

Purpose. Two reconciliations, both necessary. Path-to-path: Gold snapshot versus consumption semantic layer (same Databricks Metric Views compile) across the 1–2 programme pilots. And new-to-current: Store values versus what existing reports show today.

The second is the politically important one. Every difference from a number leadership currently sees must be explained — usually as a definitional correction, occasionally as a defect. An unexplained difference undermines the whole store.

Output. A difference register with a stated cause per item, and a sponsor decision on any that represent a change to a published figure.

CK-A Approval forum · CK-H Handover Half day eachK6

CK-A — approval. The governance act. Per KPI: the owner confirms the target, Threshold, cadence, and action, and accepts accountability for those. Meaning is already signed at G3/G4 CV/CQ — the owner does not author it. Recorded formally. This is rung 7, and it is a different kind of event from every session before it.

CK-H — handover and operating model. How a new KPI gets onboarded, how a certified formula changes, how restatement works, who runs the reconciliation harness, what happens when it fails. This is handover, not a course. Tested, not presented — a client steward completes one onboarding unaided.

15The kickoff ask

Liftable into the CK1 invitation or pre-read.

What we need from you to move from inventory to certified catalog

In the room at CK1 — Must: the executive sponsor, the enterprise data governance lead, a lead from each in-scope business area, the data platform lead, the enterprise architect, the Store lead, the formula author, the Domain Modeling Lead, the Constraints coordinating owner, and the ontologist. Domain Modeling Lead is Must. If an AI or agent initiative exists, its lead as well.

Ratified by week three: the enterprise measurement taxonomy and the KPI classification criteria. Nothing can be classified defensibly until these are the published standard.

Named — and this is the one that determines the outcome: a Business KPI Owner for each candidate KPI. A named individual, not a department, who will own the target, the Threshold, the review cadence, and the defined action. They do not author meaning — the ontology does, signed at G3/G4 CV/CQ. Without owners, the project produces an excellent register and an empty Store catalog.

A governance forum that can perform a recorded approval act, with dates in the calendar before the build completes.

Subject matter experts per business area: roughly three to five ninety-minute sessions each across a quarter, to confirm classifications and validate definitions for their own area. We will book the entire series up front.

Access in week one: Databricks and Unity Catalog, the Power BI estate, and — importantly — execution access so we can run calculations against real data to prove which ones are genuinely equivalent.

Already locked, to be held not re-litigated: C-10 (ontology = meaning; Store + consumption semantic layer = certified formula; Silver = one-way projection). C-11 (never “semantic layer” unqualified). D-M18 / DP-11 (LimitValue pattern; Constraint ≠ Threshold ≠ Breach ≠ Binding). DP-16 (formula-authoring home). DP-18 (Hamid slice gate before K4.8). Compiler tool (Hamid, 2026-08-14): programme = Databricks Metric Views; demo 1 = MetricFlow. Azure is the actual-project estate, not a demo host. G5 / K5.1 remains 1–2 Store pilots.

CK1 opening position The estate has an inventory of every calculation in the migrated reports. It does not yet have a Store catalog. The gap is ownership of target, Threshold, cadence, and action — and a signed meaning for every term — not effort. CK1 agrees how the inventory is sorted, who stands behind what is certified, and that figure and process KPIs stay out of the Store.
