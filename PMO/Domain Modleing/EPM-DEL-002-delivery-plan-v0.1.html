The honest answer to "how many workshops will we need" is one formula. Twelve sessions are fixed (listed from the §04 table). Five variables add the rest. CW1 resolves the variables and we publish the number after CW1. Header, badges, and formula use the same 22–28 range.

The planning stance to take with the client

Do not present a single invented count at CW1. Present the structure — twelve fixed, the rest driven by five variables — and the formula whose low/high is 22–28. CW1’s output is the confirmed number and calendar. This is both accurate and reassuring: it shows the plan responds to their reality rather than being imposed on it.

Two sequencing rules that protect the schedule

Assessment gates boundaries. P1 must complete before P2's boundary work, because boundaries drawn without knowing what you inherit get redrawn afterwards.
Standards publish before assessment scoring. The conformance rubric is only defensible against a published standard. This means part of P2 — specifically MOD-003 — pulls forward into P1, or the rubric is explicitly provisional and re-scored. Choose deliberately; do not discover it mid-assessment.

Sizing formula — the only count in this document

Total = 12 fixed + channels(3–5) + families(4–6) + inherited(2–3) + binding-and-trace(1–2)

05CW1 — kickoff, scope, and stakeholder identification

The only workshop that is certain, and the one that determines the shape of everything after it. Specified in full because it should be run from a script, not improvised.

What CW1 must produce

  1. Scope confirmed in writing, including the depth question and the ontology commitment
  2. Both domains bounded at a level sufficient to plan against — not modeled, bounded
  3. Q-02 must-leave-with: O2C and P2P interface dates and expected deliverable form. Do not leave the room without dates
  4. Q-03 must-leave-with: is the ontology a client-owned deliverable or an internal design technique? The answer branches the attendee pack (client-owned → platform ops, support, handover training in the room; internal technique → narrower modeling audience)
  5. TechnologyConstraint in-or-out for the Constraints domain
  6. Nominated names against every requested role, with commitment to confirm by CW2 — including Store lead and KPI owners
  7. The inherited model landscape: what exists, governance status, author availability
  8. Access requests understood and owned, with target dates
  9. Values for the five sizing variables, so the workshop schedule — and the 22–28 number — can be published
What CW1 must not do

No modeling. Not one entity, not one whiteboard box. The moment a room starts modeling in a kickoff, the scope questions go unanswered and the first structure drawn becomes an anchor nobody revisits. If the room pushes toward content — and a technical audience will — redirect with "that's exactly what CW11 is for, and today is how we make sure the right people are in it."

The questions to actually ask

Bring these written down. Ambiguity in a kickoff is almost always caused by the question never being asked plainly.

Scope
  1. Is the scope two domains at full depth, or two domains at the depth needed by O2C and P2P?
  2. Is the ontology a deliverable you will own and operate, or a technique we use internally? Q-03 — must leave with an answer. Client-owned branches the attendee pack (platform ops, support, handover training); internal technique keeps the pack to modelers and the architect
  3. What is the contracted timeline, and is it fixed-date or fixed-scope?
  4. Is physical implementation in scope at all, or do we stop at logical with one illustration?
Customer domain
  1. Which of the six channels — B2B, wholesale/rack, retail, lubricants, aviation, marine — are in scope, and are any of them small enough to fold in rather than treat separately?
  2. Which geographies or legal entities? Tax and excise structures are jurisdiction-specific and they are core customer attributes here.
  3. Is the retail end consumer — loyalty member, card holder — in scope, or only the dealer?
  4. Is there an MDM or customer master programme running, and what is its scope?
  5. Is SAP accessible to this project? If not, why not, and can that change?
  6. Who could be the accountable owner for customer definitions across all channels?
Constraints domain
  1. One refinery or several? Multiple sites multiply specifications and permits substantially.
  2. Which constraint families are material — unit operability, product spec, blending, tankage, movements, crude, regulatory, maintenance, contractual, utilities?
  3. Where do constraints currently live authoritatively — the LP model, spreadsheets, procedures, permits, or people's heads?
  4. Can we get access to the LP planning model and the specification library?
  5. Who could plausibly coordinate this domain, given that no one person owns every constraint?
  6. Are KPI thresholds and management targets in scope, or only operational and technical limits? Agree here that they are not the same object as a Constraint — LimitValue pattern is shared; Constraint ≠ Threshold ≠ Breach ≠ Binding (D-M18)
  7. Is TechnologyConstraint in or out — control-system, historian, ISA-95, digital limits as a first-class subtype, not a property of Operational?
Consuming programmes
  1. Q-02. When does O2C need the Customer model, and in what form — a document, a model file, published glossary terms, physical structures? Must leave with a date
  2. Q-02. When does P2P need the Constraints model, and in what form? Must leave with a date
  3. What happens to those programmes if we are late? Understanding the real consequence shapes how we sequence.
  4. Can we deliver in stages rather than one handover?
Inherited models
  1. Was the Party model formally approved by a business owner, and is that recorded anywhere?
  2. What consumes it today — which reports, which teams, which downstream processes?
  3. Why was SAP out of scope for that work — access, timing, or a deliberate boundary?
  4. What is the governance status of the Finance and Supply Chain models?
  5. Are the original teams available for walkthroughs, and who specifically?
  6. Is there a standing agreement to consume their deliverables, or is that our working assumption?
People, access, and governance
  1. Who is our single day-to-day client counterpart?
  2. Is there an existing governance forum that can ratify standards, or do we need to create a review board?
  3. What is the typical lead time for access provisioning here?
  4. Does a graph platform already exist and who operates it?
  5. Are there site access, shift patterns, or turnaround windows that constrain when refinery staff can attend workshops?
  6. What does success look like to the sponsor in six months? Ask it plainly and write the answer down verbatim.

06CW2 — stakeholder confirmation and ownership commitment

CW1 identifies; CW2 confirms. The gap between them exists so nominees can be approached and can agree — a name volunteered in a kickoff by someone else is not a commitment.

Why this is a separate session

Nominating someone in a room they are not in produces a list, not a team. CW2 exists to convert nominations into accepted commitments, in the presence of the sponsor, with the time cost stated out loud. It is short, slightly uncomfortable, and it is the single most reliable predictor of whether the models will ever reach approved status.

The test of a successful CW2

Every role has a name, that person knows they hold it, their manager knows the time cost, and the whole workshop series is in their calendar. If any of those four is missing for the Customer domain owner or the Constraints coordinating owner, that is an amber status in the first steering report — not a detail to resolve quietly.

07CW3 onward

The remaining workshops, grouped by track. Expand for purpose, attendees, and the specific output each must produce.

CW3–CW5 Inherited model walkthroughs Half day eachP1

Purpose. Present desk-review findings to each originating team as questions, and revise scores where the answers warrant it. Many apparent defects are documented decisions taken for a reason; some are not.

Attendees. Original modeling team, their lead, our modelers and Domain Modeling Lead, Enterprise Data Governance as observer.

Conduct. Open by presenting our own Party model scorecard, including its poor scores. Then lead with what we are adopting, specifically, before moving to gaps. Cite evidence — the entity, the attribute — never impressions.

Output. Revised scores, with a record of which scores the walkthrough changed. That record is the evidence the review was genuine.

If the team is unavailable — proceed on desk review, score unverifiable dimensions as Not assessable, which counts against adoption.

CW6 Modeling standards and conventions Full dayP2

Purpose. Ratify naming, keys, levels, relationships, definition quality, required metadata, versioning.

Attendees. Our modelers and ontologist, ER/Studio admin, Purview owner, Enterprise Data Governance, one steward per domain, enterprise architect.

Method. Test every convention live against ten real entities — five from Customer, five from Constraints. The two domains stress conventions differently, which is exactly why both are needed in the room.

Timebox. Ninety minutes on naming, then the Domain Modeling Lead decides and records it. Naming attracts debate far out of proportion to its decision value.

CW7 Ontology design, tooling, and CQ method Full dayP2

Split the day. (a) is the gate; (b) is optional and is not a G2 gate.

(a) MOD-006 / IRI / modules / SHACL+OWL + CQ method. Lock naming and IRI policy, modularisation (shared upper, Customer, Constraints), vocabulary reuse, SHACL + OWL validation, and how competency questions will be written and signed — before a single triple is written. This is what G2 actually needs. CW7 may preview the CQ method. Business CQs are written at P2.6 and signed at CV/CQ sign-off (Customer after CW11 s1–2; Constraints at CW18). CW7 is not CQ sign-off.

(b) Optional platform spike. A sandbox triple store if it helps the team learn. Local files are sufficient. G2 does not require a running triple store.

Attendees. Our ontologist and backend engineers, enterprise architect, Enterprise Data Governance, Domain Modeling Lead. Platform operations only if (b) is in play; Q-03 (client-owned vs internal technique) branches whether they must be in the room.

Also. Preview the CQ method with one or two worked examples so the room sees the fitness test — do not walk or sign the domain CQ sets here. Those are written at P2.6 and signed at CV/CQ sign-off.

CW8 · CW9 Domain boundaries Half day eachP2

CW8 — Customer. Where does customer end and product, contract, finance, or order begin? Is the motorist in? Is identity resolution in? Are dealer agreements customer or asset? Attendees: Domain Data Owner, channel representatives, MDM, credit, finance representative.

CW9 — Constraints. Does the domain own the constraint only, or the constrained object too? Recommended: the constraint only, with a typed reference to the object. Also settle the federated ownership structure and whether targets and thresholds are in scope. Attendees: coordinating owner candidate, all family steward candidates, LP planner, Global Process Owner.

Both. Test the boundary rule against three deliberately contested cases. A boundary rule that has not been tested against a hard case has not been tested.

CW10 Tool responsibility and metadata boundaries Half dayP2

Purpose. Establish one authoring home per fact type across six locations plus three Store seats — ontology, ER/Studio, Purview, Unity Catalog, Silver, Excel, plus Store metadata dimension, consumption semantic layer, and Gold fact.

Attendees. All platform owners, our ontologist and backend engineers, Enterprise Data Governance, enterprise architect, Must: KPI Store lead.

Four things to settle. The Excel containment rule — staging only, provenance flagged, migration target named. Silver as a one-way projection of the Store catalog plus ontology IRIs, not editable. The C-10 split: ontology = SoT for what the term means; Store + consumption semantic layer = SoT for how the certified number is computed; Gold = published volumes. Never say “semantic layer” unqualified. And the formula-authoring home — named before CW10 closes; the Store/analytics engineer is the formula author.

CW11–CW14 Customer conceptual modeling series Half day eachP3A

Attendees. Domain Data Owner, one representative per in-scope channel, credit, tax and excise, our facilitator and scribe, BA, steward.

Session 1 — sense inventory / UL freeze of the six terms. Not “what is a customer, per channel.” Freeze canonical + aliases + includes/excludes for Customer Account, Sold-To, Bill-To, Payer, Ship-To (location, not a role), and Loading-Authorized Party (≠ the Authorization object). CreditAccount is first-class; credit lives on the payer. Collect channel usage before reconciling; the differences are the content. Harvest is a sense-inventory, not a label-copy of “Customer.”

Session 2 — roles and hierarchies. Who plays what role, in what context, over what period? Which groupings exist and are they the same? Expect at least three distinct hierarchies; treat a claim that there is only one as a finding to investigate. Party AND Customer Account + roles.

CV / CQ sign-off after sessions 1–2 — a real gate. Domain Data Owner — Customer signs the controlled vocabulary and the competency questions; the ontologist scribes. Do not proceed to sessions 3–4 without that signature. P3A.3 (triples / classes) and G3 depend on signed Customer CV/CQ — not workshop prose only.

Session 3 — agreements and obligations. Contracts, pricing, rebates, commitments, credit structure, compliance status.

Session 4 — challenge. Walk the competency questions. Any unanswerable one means a missing concept, fixed in the room.

Facilitation. No ER notation in session 1. Boxes and plain verbs. And do not show the existing Party model until after session 2 — anchoring is a real and costly risk.

CW15 Party, role, hierarchy and identity deep dive Full dayP3A

Purpose. Settle the structural decision that determines the entire Customer model: party-plus-role versus customer-as-entity, and how many hierarchies exist.

Method that works. Bring five real organisations that play multiple roles across channels. Model each live under both structures and let the room watch one of them lose information. The party/role case makes itself when the alternative is shown failing on a real example — argument alone rarely lands.

Also settle. The identity-resolution scope line: concepts modeled, matching rules not built. Say it explicitly and write it down, or MDM arrives by stealth.

CW16 · CW24 Source binding walkthroughs Half day eachP3A / P3B

CW16 — Customer. SAP functional lead, CRM owner, credit system owner, card platform owner, Databricks engineer. Expect the same party under different identifiers across systems with no reliable join key — that is a finding, not a failure.

CW24 — Constraints. LP planner, scheduler, quality/lab, tank farm, maintenance planner. Expect values held in planning model files rather than databases, and unit and reference-condition differences between systems — ambient versus standard volume being the classic case, and one that quietly corrupts yield and loss figures downstream.

Rule for both. Record gaps explicitly. A binding session that produces no gaps has not looked hard enough.

CW17 · CW25 Model review gates 3 hours eachP3A / P3B

Purpose. The formal promotion act, and the dry run of the gate the playbook will prescribe for every future domain.

Attendees. Domain Data Owner (decides), Enterprise Data Governance, EPM Lead, our Domain Modeling Lead and ontologist, and a reviewer who was not in the modeling workshops.

Agenda. Independent reviewer presents findings first · shared-meaning checklist before any reasoner or SPARQL run (every signed CV/CQ term still means what was signed; no silent rename) · definition-of-done checklist · competency questions answered against the graph, not asserted · validation output shown, not described · disposition every finding · promotion decision.

Why the outside reviewer. Everyone who built the model has been anchored by the discussion. Someone who was not there is the only reliable test of whether the definitions stand alone.

CW18 Constraint taxonomy Full dayP3B

Purpose. First CV freeze, then CQs, then is-a ratification. Do not ratify the is-a class tree in the same breath as the first CV freeze. Write Constraint ≠ Threshold ≠ Breach ≠ Binding as a governance definition. This gates the entire Constraints build; without it every subsequent session re-litigates the categories.

Agenda — order is load-bearing, even on the same day. (1) CV — preferred term + aliases + includes/excludes for Constraint, Threshold, Breach, Binding, and the family names. (2) CQs — confirm the competency questions written at P2.6. (3) Then is-a ratification — Operational vs Technology as the only class split, families under those two subtypes, and the four property axes (authority, hardness, horizon, constrained object).

Attendees. Coordinating owner, all family stewards, LP planner, process engineer, quality, HSE, maintenance, commercial. OT / historian / ISA-95 if Technology is in.

Method. After the CV and CQs, classify ~20 real constraints from the empirical inventory live. Resolve the ones that will not classify cleanly. Confirm LimitValue is the shared pattern and that Store KPIs reference constraints rather than subclassing them.

CV / CQ sign-off at the end of CW18 — a real gate. Coordinating Domain Data Owner signs; ontologist scribes. Do not open CW19 without that signature. P3B.4 (triples / classes) and G4 depend on signed Constraints CV/CQ — not workshop prose only.

Expect friction over hard versus soft. That disagreement is the most valuable output of the day — it usually means the constraint is being enforced differently by different teams right now.

CW19–CW23 Constraints conceptual series, by family cluster Half day eachP3B

Structure. Group by family cluster rather than running one large room — unit and utilities · specification and blending · tankage and movement · regulatory and maintenance · contractual. Different people, different vocabulary, same underlying structure.

Method that works here. Start from the empirical constraint inventory and ask "how would we express this one?" Building a constraint model from first principles in an empty room produces an elegant structure that nothing actually fits.

Watch for. Constraints that are really targets. Constraints whose stated value differs between the LP model and operations. Constraints nobody can name an authority for — usually the most interesting ones in the room.

CW26 · CW27 · CW28 Validation, traceability, and close Half day eachP4 / P5

CW26 — ontology validation review. Shared-meaning checklist first — every signed CV/CQ term still means what was signed — then reasoner and SPARQL. Classify every inconsistency as a model defect or a genuine business contradiction. Execute every competency question live. This is where the ontology either demonstrates value beyond the ERD or demonstrably does not.

CW27 — KPI traceability binding. 1–2 certified Store pilots, not three process metrics. For each pilot, record customer_grain (payer / sold-to / site). Figure and process KPIs are not Store-certified. Confirm the LimitValue pattern is reused and the ≠ rule holds — Store KPIs reference constraints; they do not subclass them. Routinely exposes KPIs that cannot be computed as defined, which is worth more than the mapping itself. KPI Store lead is Must; KPI owner per pilot is Must; Store/analytics engineer (formula author) is Must.

CW28 — lessons learned and close. Correct the playbook. Build the effort model. Present the scale recommendation including the do-not-scale-yet option. Most useful question of the day: "where did we quietly not follow the playbook?"

16The kickoff ask

A summary you can lift directly into the CW1 invitation or the pre-read.

What we need from you to start

In the room at CW1: the executive sponsor, the O2C and P2P programme leads, a commercial leadership representative, a refinery technical or planning leadership representative, the enterprise data governance lead, and the enterprise architect.

Named by CW2 — decision roles: a Domain Data Owner for Customer with authority across all in-scope channels; a coordinating Domain Data Owner for Operational Constraints who owns the model's structure rather than every constraint's value; an enterprise data governance lead to ratify standards; a Global Process Owner (or drop the RACI row at CW1 and give disputes to the sponsor); and a single day-to-day counterpart.

Named by CW2 — subject matter experts: one representative per in-scope commercial channel, plus credit, tax and excise, the customer master steward, and the TAS / terminal-auth SME. On the operations side: the refinery planner or LP economist, a scheduler, a process engineer, product quality, HSE and permitting, maintenance planning, tank farm and movements, commercial contracts, and OT / historian / DQ / ISA-95 (Technology in-or-out is a CW1 decision).

Named by CW2 — Store and formula: the KPI Store lead (Should at CW1; Must at CW10 and CW27); a KPI owner per Store pilot; and the Store / analytics engineer (formula author), named before CW10.

Access, requested in week one: ER/Studio, Purview, Unity Catalog, the LP planning system and specification library, and — critically for the Customer domain — SAP, together with a functional lead who can explain the business partner structure.

Availability: approximately four hours per week from each domain owner during their domain's build phase, and two to four hours per workshop from each subject matter expert across roughly twenty sessions. We will publish the full calendar within a week of CW1 so that diaries can be blocked early — refinery shift patterns and turnaround windows constrain this more than office schedules do.

The sentence to open CW1 with "Today we are not modeling anything. We are agreeing what is in scope, who owns which decisions, and what we already have that we can build on — so that when we do start modeling, the right people are in the room and we are not redoing it in three months."
