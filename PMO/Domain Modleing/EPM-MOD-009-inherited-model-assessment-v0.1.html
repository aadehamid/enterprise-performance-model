01Why this exists

Three model assets exist upstream of the Customer and Constraints domains. All three are useful. None has been assessed. Deciding what to do with them is now the first substantive task in the workstream, ahead of any modeling.

The temptation with an inherited model is binary: adopt it because it exists, or ignore it because it wasn't built here. Both are expensive. Adopting an unassessed model imports its defects and then blesses them with your project's approval status. Ignoring one throws away real agreed meaning and guarantees a turf fight when the two models disagree in front of a stakeholder.

The middle path needs three things this document supplies: stated criteria, so the assessment is a measurement rather than an opinion; a disposition framework, so the assessment produces a decision rather than a report; and a conduct standard, so reviewing a peer team's work does not become a political event.

The one-sentence purpose

Turn "we should probably look at what they built" into a three-week task with a defined output, a named decider, and a written disposition per subject area (D-M22) — not per model.

Why the Party model's three characteristics matter

Each one, independently, predicts a specific structural shape. Together they are diagnostic. This is the hypothesis the assessment tests — not a conclusion.

CharacteristicWhat it usually producesConsequence for full-depth Customer modeling
Scoped to reporting needs Only concepts that appeared on a dashboard exist. Credit structure, compliance status, contract depth, and authorization relationships are typically absent because nothing reported on them. Extension is not additive. The absent concepts are not leaves hanging off the existing structure — several of them are structural, and adding them changes the shape of what is already there.
Organised by data portfolio, not domain Grouping follows ownership or consumption rather than meaning. Entities that belong together semantically may sit in different portfolios, and unrelated entities may share one. The two organising principles must be reconciled explicitly. They should coexist as a mapping, not be forced to align — see decision D-M20.
SAP not in scope The model was built from downstream copies of customer data rather than from the master. Copies carry the warehouse's grain, its flattening, and its historical decisions. The most significant of the three. A customer model that never met the customer master is modeling a shadow. SAP business partner structure — partner functions, roles, hierarchies, account groups — is precisely the material Domain 1 needs.
Stated plainly, and without prejudice to the assessment

A reporting-scoped, portfolio-grouped model built without the master is not a bad model. It is a model built correctly for a different job. The question this framework answers is whether that job's structure can carry this job's weight — and the honest expectation is that its definitions will transfer well and its structure may not.

03The inheritance rule

One rule, stated before any assessment begins, so that it cannot look like a reaction to a finding.

Inheritance confers no approval status

A model arriving from another project or another team enters this workstream as evidence, at the source-precedence tier its own governance history earns it — and no higher. Delivery is not approval. Recency is not authority. Being ours is not conformance.

How to tier an inherited model

TierApplies whenWhat it entitles the model to
3 Approved specificationThe model was formally approved by an accountable business owner through a governance act, and that act is documentedCannot be overridden silently. A conflict must be logged and escalated, not resolved by the modeler.
4 Validated implementationThe model is physically implemented, in production, and reconciles to a validated sourceStrong evidence of what the data actually does. Not evidence of what the business means.
7 Prior or outside-project workDelivered but not formally approved — the most likely tier for all three assetsBaseline evidence. Reusable after assessment. Overridable with rationale recorded.

The first assessment question for every inherited model is therefore not "is it any good" but "was it ever approved, by whom, and is that recorded?" The answer determines whether the rest of the assessment is a review or a negotiation.

Why stating this first protects everyone If the rule is published before the first model is opened, a low score reads as a measurement against known criteria. If it is invoked after a low score, it reads as a justification. Same rule, entirely different reception.

04Conformance rubric

Twenty-one dimensions (A1–A5, B1–B8, C1–C4, D1–D4). Each has a stated "what good looks like" and a warning sign that can be checked without interpretation. Score every model on every dimension, including our own Party model — the rubric loses its authority the moment it is applied selectively.

ConformantMeets the criterion without qualification.
PartialMet for some of the model, or met in substance but not consistently.
Non-conformantNot met. Record the specific evidence.
Not assessableCannot be judged from what is available. Usually a finding in itself.

Group A — Foundations weighted highest

Failures here are structural. They are expensive or impossible to remediate without rebuilding, and they should dominate the disposition decision.

#DimensionWhat good looks likeWarning sign
A1Level disciplineThe model states its level, and its content matches. A logical model contains no platform artifacts; a conceptual model contains no attributes.Data types, index hints, staging tables, or surrogate keys appearing in something labelled logical or conceptual
A2Meaning-driven structureEntities exist because the business distinguishes them, not because a report or a source table did.Entity names matching source table names one-for-one; entities that exist only because a dashboard needed a join
A3Role and entity separationA party plays several roles; Sold-To / Bill-To / Payer (and vendor, carrier) are roles, not separate parties. Customer Account is the account, not a role synonym. Do not overload “Customer.”Separate Customer and Vendor entities with duplicated name and address attributes and no shared party concept
A4Identity and keysBusiness keys are identified and distinguished from technical keys. Cross-system identity is handled explicitly.Only source-system surrogate keys; no statement of what identifies the thing in business terms
A5Scope honestyThe model documents what it does not cover, and why.A model titled for a whole domain whose actual coverage is whatever was in scope for one delivery, with no exclusions stated

Group B — Semantics

Failures here are remediable but labour-intensive, and remediation needs business time rather than modeler time — which is usually the harder resource to secure.

#DimensionWhat good looks likeWarning sign
B1Definition presenceEvery entity and attribute carries a business definition.Blank definition fields; definitions only on the entities someone asked about
B2Definition qualityDefinitions say what the thing is and how to tell it from adjacent things. Canonical + aliases + includes/excludes, not a restatement of the label."Customer — a customer." Restating the name is the most common failure and the easiest to spot. Harvest is a sense-inventory, not a label-copy of “Customer”
B3Approval provenanceEach definition names who approved it and when.Definitions authored by the modeling team with no business sign-off recorded
B4Governed value setsCode sets and classifications are enumerated, owned, and governed.Free-text status and type attributes; values discoverable only by querying the data
B5Aliases / UF-USEEvery preferred term records its aliases and use-for / use relations. Channel language is captured, not flattened.One label, no aliases; “customer” used for account, payer, and site interchangeably
B6Includes / excludesEach core term states what is in and what is out, in one sentence each.No exclusion line; Ship-To treated as a role; Loading-Authorized Party collapsed into Authorization
B7Homonym registerKnown collisions (“loss”, “customer”, “limit”) are listed with the two meanings and the owning communities.The same word used for Constraint and Threshold with no note
B8Competency questions as fitnessCQs exist and are the fitness test for the harvested meaning — not a documentation appendix.No CQs, or CQs written after the model to describe what was already drawn

Group C — Structure and behaviour

#DimensionWhat good looks likeWarning sign
C1Relationship integrityCardinality and optionality stated and defensible; many-to-many resolved with a named associative concept.Unresolved many-to-many; relationships with no verb phrase; cardinality that contradicts a known business case
C2TemporalityEffectivity, versioning, and history are modeled where the business needs them, not left to the platform.No effective dating anywhere; "current state only" with no statement that history was deliberately excluded
C3Hierarchy treatmentHierarchies are named, typed structures. Multiple coexisting hierarchies are supported.A single parent-child column on the main entity, implying exactly one hierarchy exists
C4Naming consistencyA stated convention, applied throughout.Mixed conventions across subject areas, indicating several hands and no standard

Group D — Traceability and reuse

#DimensionWhat good looks likeWarning sign
D1Source traceabilityEntities and attributes trace to source systems and physical assets, with transformations noted.No mapping, or mapping only to the warehouse rather than to the originating system
D2Master-source coverageThe model was built against the systems that master the data, not only against downstream copies.The system of record for the domain's core entity was out of scope — the Party model's stated SAP gap sits here
D3Consumer independenceThe model would be recognisable and useful to a consumer other than the one it was built for.Structure that only makes sense given one report's requirements; measures embedded as attributes
D4Glossary linkageEntities and attributes link to governed business terms.Definitions living only inside the modeling tool, unreachable from the catalog
How to read a completed scorecard

Group A failures point toward rebuild or harvest — they are shape problems. Groups B, C, and D failures point toward adopt with remediation — they are completeness and rigour problems, painful but additive. A model can score poorly overall and still be worth adopting if the poor scores cluster in B and D. A model can score reasonably overall and still need rebuilding if it fails A2, A3, or D2.

Deliberate omission There is no numeric total. A weighted score invites arguing about weights instead of about evidence, and it lets three amber scores in Group A average away behind good scores elsewhere. The disposition rule in §06 reads the pattern, not the sum.

05The Party model — four questions that decide extend versus rebuild

The full rubric applies. But four dimensions decide the disposition of this specific model, and they can be answered in a single afternoon with the model open.

#QuestionExtend-compatible answerRebuild-indicating answer
P1 Is Party separate from role? Does one organisation playing several roles appear once, or several times? Probe: is Ship-To a location or a role? Does credit sit on the payer (CreditAccount first-class) or on a generic Customer? A Party concept exists with roles attached as relationships. Ship-To is a location. CreditAccount is on the payer. Customer Account is first-class; Sold-To / Bill-To / Payer are roles Customer is itself an entity; the same organisation as a carrier appears as a different record; Ship-To is modeled as a partner function / role; credit hangs off “Customer”
P2 How are hierarchies modeled? A named, typed hierarchy structure supporting more than one hierarchy A parent-customer column, or a flattened level-1 / level-2 / level-3 set of attributes
P3 Whose grain is the model at? Entities exist at the grain the business distinguishes them Entities exist at the grain the reports aggregated to; dimensional shapes visible in a logical model
P4 Was it built against the master or a copy? Traced to the mastering system's structures Traced only to warehouse tables — which the stated SAP exclusion makes the likely answer
Decision rule Tier 9 — recommended, not approved

Apply this mapping after the four-question test is scored. Do not pre-commit a disposition.
Three or four rebuild-indicating answers → harvest (sense-inventory) and rebuild structure. The structure cannot carry full domain depth; the agreed meaning can and should be carried forward as a sense-inventory, not a label-copy of “Customer.”
One or two → adopt with remediation. Restructure the specific failures, keep the rest.
None → extend. Add the missing concepts and the SAP-sourced material to the existing model.

What "harvest and rebuild" actually preserves

The phrase sounds like throwing work away. It is not — and being precise about this matters both technically and politically, because the team that built it is your own.

Carried forwardRebuilt
Every business definition that has been agreed · the vocabulary the business actually uses · the data portfolio mapping, as a consumption view · known source-system field mappings · documented data-quality findings · the list of what consumers currently depend on · every hard-won piece of knowledge about where the data is dirty Entity structure and grain · role treatment · hierarchy structures · key strategy · the concepts that reporting never needed · everything SAP-sourced
The mapping obligation

If the structure is rebuilt, an old-to-new mapping is mandatory, not optional. There is a live consumption estate on the existing model. A rebuild that orphans it converts a modeling improvement into a reporting outage, and that is how modeling programmes acquire a reputation. Budget the mapping as part of the rebuild, not as a follow-on.

The portfolio-versus-domain question

The existing model groups by data portfolio; this workstream groups by domain. These are different organising principles answering different questions, and the instinct to make them align should be resisted.

Data portfolioDomain
AnswersWho owns and supplies this data, and who consumes itWhat does the business mean by this, and who decides
Boundary driven byOwnership, delivery, and consumptionCoherent meaning and a single accountable approver
Changes whenOwnership or platform structure changesThe business's understanding changes — much less often
Recommended treatmentKeep both; map many-to-many; do not force alignment. Retiring portfolios breaks the consumption estate. Forcing domains to match portfolios reintroduces the ownership-shaped boundaries that domain modeling exists to escape. The mapping is the deliverable — decision D-M20.

06Disposition framework

Five outcomes. Every assessed model gets exactly one, recorded, with rationale. An assessment that does not end in a disposition is a report, and reports do not unblock modeling.

DispositionUse whenWhat it obligatesWhat it forecloses
Adopt Conformant across Group A, with at most minor gaps elsewhere Bring into the domain model repository under our artifact control; register provenance; assume maintenance responsibility or agree a shared one Rebuilding it later without a decision record
Adopt with remediation Group A broadly sound; failures cluster in B, C, or D A scoped, estimated, owned remediation backlog with a completion gate before the model is used downstream Treating it as usable before remediation completes
Harvest Group A failures make the structure unusable, but definitions, mappings, and domain knowledge are valuable Extract semantics with attribution; build the old-to-new mapping; tell the original team what was carried forward and why Claiming the original model as the baseline for the new one
Reference Not usable as a model input, but valuable as evidence of current state, consumer needs, or data reality Register as a source; cite where it informs a decision; do not import structure Any implication that its content is governed
Reject Neither structure nor semantics is usable, or the model addresses something out of scope Record the rationale formally. A rejection without a written reason is re-litigated every quarter Nothing — but the record prevents the same debate recurring

Disposition is per subject area, not per model

A Finance model may hold an excellent receivables structure and a poor tax structure. Forcing one disposition across a whole model either imports the weak part or discards the strong one. Assess and dispose at the subject-area level, and record the disposition set rather than a single verdict. This also considerably reduces the political temperature — "we're adopting your receivables model and rebuilding tax" lands very differently from "your model failed."

The default that saves time

When genuinely torn between adopt with remediation and harvest, choose harvest. Remediating someone else's model requires their availability, their agreement, and their change control, on their schedule. Harvesting requires only a mapping. Remediation is the right choice when the model has a live consuming estate that must not break — which is exactly the Party model's situation, and precisely why that one needs the four-question test rather than a default.

Two timeboxes worth enforcing

The desk review is capped. The goal is a disposition, not a defect catalogue. Once enough evidence exists to decide, stop scoring and decide — further findings change nothing.
The relevance filter is applied before the desk review, not after. Reviewing an entire Finance model to discover that four subject areas were relevant is the single easiest way to lose a month.

08Awkward cases

Four situations that will arise, with a position on each decided in advance — because deciding them in the moment tends to favour whoever is most insistent.

A needed concept exists only in a non-conformant model Most likely

The situation. Constraints needs tankage structure. It exists in the Supply Chain model. That model scores badly on Group A. Rebuilding it is out of scope; waiting is not an option.

Position. Harvest the concept, restructure it to our standard, and record the provenance and the divergence explicitly. Do not import the structure to save time, and do not block the domain waiting for someone else's remediation cycle.

What must be recorded. That our version diverges from theirs, why, and which is authoritative for which purpose. Two structures for the same concept is survivable if documented; it is corrosive if discovered later by a stakeholder.

The originating team disputes the assessment Likely

Position. Disputes are about evidence against a published criterion, never about the criterion being applied. If they disagree with a score, they point to the entity or attribute that contradicts it and the score changes. If they disagree with the criterion itself, that escalates to Enterprise Data Governance as a standards question — not as a review outcome.

The structural protection. Publish EPM-MOD-003 and this rubric before assessing. Then the conversation is "does this model meet the standard" rather than "is your opinion better than mine." Publishing the criteria first is the single highest-leverage thing you can do to keep this from becoming political.

Escalation. Enterprise Data Governance on criteria; Global Process Owner on cross-domain boundary disputes; executive sponsor only on resourcing consequences.

The originating team is unavailable or disbanded Likely for the Party model

Position. Proceed with the desk review and mark step 4 as not performed. Every dimension that could not be assessed without the authors is scored Not assessable, which counts against adoption rather than for it — an unverifiable model cannot be adopted into a governed baseline.

Practical mitigation. Find one person who was in the room, even peripherally. Thirty minutes with someone who remembers why a decision was taken is worth several days of inference.

The inherited model has a live consumption estate Certain, for the Party model

Position. A live estate does not change the assessment — it changes the transition. Score honestly; then, if the disposition is harvest or rebuild, an old-to-new mapping and a migration path become mandatory deliverables of the rebuild, budgeted inside it.

What to avoid. Softening the assessment because migration looks expensive. That trades a known one-time cost for an unbounded ongoing one, and it means the structural defect is now inside your approved baseline with your name on it.

09Conducting a peer review

A conformance assessment of a peer team's work is a political act whether or not anyone intends it to be. Six practices that keep it professional. Tier 9.

PracticeWhy
Publish the criteria before opening a single modelTurns every finding into a measurement. Criteria produced after a bad score always look retrofitted, however honestly they were arrived at.
Assess our own Party model first, and share that scorecardNothing else buys as much goodwill. It demonstrates the rubric is a standard rather than a weapon — and our model will score imperfectly, which is the point.
Frame findings as fitness for this purpose"Built for a different job" is accurate, true, and non-adversarial. "Doesn't follow best practice" is a judgement about competence, lands as one, and is usually less accurate.
Cite evidence, never impressions"Entity X has no business key" is discussable. "The model feels source-driven" is an argument with no exit.
Send each team their own sections before wider circulationNobody should learn their model was assessed from a steering deck. This one practice prevents most escalations.
Name what you are adopting, first and specificallyLead with what carries forward. A review that opens with adoption and moves to gaps is received completely differently from the same content in the reverse order.
The framing that works

"We are building customer to full domain depth including SAP, which was outside your scope. We want to carry forward as much of your model as we can, so we are assessing it against the standard we will hold our own work to — and we have scored our own Party model first." That sentence is accurate, it is respectful, and it makes the assessment obviously reciprocal.

P1 / CW3–CW5 gates P2 boundaries (CW8 / CW9). It is tempting to run the assessment in parallel with boundary definition to save time, but domain boundaries drawn before you know what you are inheriting get redrawn afterwards. Three weeks spent here is cheaper than redrawing boundaries and re-running CW8 / CW9.

14Glossary

TermWhat it actually means
Inherited modelAny model this workstream did not build but may use. Includes our own prior work — that is the point of the word.
ConformanceWhether a model meets a published standard. Distinct from quality: a model can be well built for a different purpose and still be non-conformant here.
DispositionThe decision about what to do with an assessed model. Five options, one per subject area, always recorded.
HarvestA sense-inventory of agreed meaning — definitions, aliases, includes/excludes, mappings, and domain knowledge — not a label-copy of “Customer.” Structure is rebuilt. Disposition is not pre-committed; the four-question test is scored first.
Data portfolioA grouping of data by who owns and consumes it. Answers "who supplies this."
DomainA grouping of data by what it means, with one accountable approver. Answers "what does this mean and who decides."
Reporting-scoped modelA model containing only what some report needed. Not a criticism — a description of what determined its boundary, and a predictor of what will be missing.
Master versus copyThe master is the system where data is created and maintained (SAP for customer, typically). A copy is a downstream replica. Modeling from a copy inherits the copy's shape rather than the business's.
GrainThe level of detail at which something is recorded. Reporting grain and business grain differ, and a model built at reporting grain cannot answer questions below it.
Incumbency authorityThe tendency of whatever exists to be treated as the standard, purely because it exists. The specific risk the §03 inheritance rule guards against.
AnchoringLetting the first structure you see shape the one you build. Mitigated by running conceptual workshops from business vocabulary before showing anyone the existing model.
Not assessableA score, not an absence of one. It counts against adoption, because a model nobody can verify cannot enter a governed baseline.
