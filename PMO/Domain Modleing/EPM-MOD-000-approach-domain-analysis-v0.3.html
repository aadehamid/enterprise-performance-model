The three levels, plainly A conceptual model answers "what things does this business talk about, and how do they relate?" — no technology at all. 04Domain 1 — Customer feeds Order-to-Cash

Analytical input for the modeling workshops, not a model. Tier 9 — industry pattern, requires validation against this organisation.

The central difficulty

In most industries "customer" is a stable idea. In Downstream it is not, for three reasons that compound:

  • The same legal entity plays many roles. A logistics firm can simultaneously be a wholesale customer, a haulage contractor, a rack carrier, and a lubricants distributor. Four customer records loses the shared credit exposure; one record loses that the roles have different owners, terms, and processes.
  • Who "the customer" is differs by channel. In aviation the paying party may be a fuel consortium rather than the airline consuming the fuel. In marine bunkering the buyer, vessel operator, and charterer can be three parties. In retail the dealer is the customer and the motorist is not — except in loyalty and card programmes, where the motorist suddenly is.
  • Regulatory status changes the economics. Excise registration, duty-suspension eligibility, and tax classification determine the price of the same physical product. Not a compliance footnote — core model content.

Customer UL — freeze before taxonomy

Canonical term + aliases + includes/excludes, then the tree. The six terms to freeze: Customer Account, Sold-To, Bill-To, Payer, Ship-To (location, not a role), and Loading-Authorized Party (≠ the Authorization object). CreditAccount is first-class; credit lives on the payer. Do not overload “Customer.”

What O2C actually needs from this model

Order capture needs to know who may order what, on what terms, to what location. Credit check needs the exposure grouping, which is rarely the ordering entity. Pricing needs contract and rebate structure. Delivery needs authorization and ship-to. Invoicing needs the payer and tax treatment. Collections needs the dunning hierarchy. Each is a different grouping of the same parties. A model producing one hierarchy is unusable by O2C.

05Domain 2 — Operational and technological constraints feeds Plan-to-Produce

A constraint is not a thing the business owns; it is a statement about what is permitted. That makes this domain structurally unlike Customer. Tier 9. “Produce” here includes buy, exchange, throughput, and terminal blend — not only make.

The central difficulty

Customer is a master data domain: things exist, have attributes, relate. Constraints is a rules domain: statements exist, parameterised, conditional, time-bound, applying to objects owned by other domains. Three consequences shape the whole approach:

  • No natural single owner. Unit capacity belongs to operations, product specification to quality and regulatory, emissions caps to HSE and permitting, contractual minimums to commercial. Applying the single-owner boundary rule, this domain fails it.
  • The authoritative value usually lives in an operational system — an LP planning model, a DCS configuration, a permit document, a specification sheet — and changes on that system's cycle. The domain model must describe constraints without claiming to be their source of truth.
  • It is the natural home of a distinction the KPI Store also needs. Constraint, target, specification, and threshold look identical in a spreadsheet and are not. Getting this right here pays twice.
D-M18 — shared LimitValue pattern, not the same object

Share an n-ary LimitValue pattern: value, unit, quantity kind, direction, reference conditions, valid-time. That is the reusable structure. The objects that use it are not the same: Constraint (standing restriction; authority = physics / permit / spec / SOL) ≠ Threshold (KPI scoring / RAG; authority = KPI owner) ≠ Breach ≠ Binding. Store KPIs reference constraints; they do not subclass them. P3B.7 / CW27 / DP-11 / CW1 Q6 / CW9: agree the pattern and the ≠ rule. Do not treat Constraint and Threshold as one object.

A constraint taxonomy to start from

Only Operational versus Technology is a class split. OperationalConstraint and TechnologyConstraint are first-class subtypes; families sit under those two. The four axes below are properties of a constraint — not further classes. Horizon, hardness, authority, and constrained object stay properties. Treating the whole space as one flat list, or promoting the axes to classes, is the most common modeling error in this space.

The ownership problem, stated plainly

No single person can approve a unit capacity limit, a sulphur specification, a flaring cap, and a take-or-pay minimum. The recommended structure is a coordinating Domain Data Owner who owns the constraint model — taxonomy, structure, lifecycle — with constraint-family stewards from operations, quality, HSE, maintenance, and commercial owning the content of their family. Decide before boundary work completes or the domain stalls at first review. Decision D-M17.

Why the two domains stress the method differently

Customer is about things and their roles over time. Constraints is about statements and their conditions. One set of modeling conventions will not fit both cleanly — which is good for proving a playbook and awkward for writing one. Test every convention against entities from both.

What P2P actually needs

Crude selection and nomination need slate and compatibility constraints. Planning needs the full constraint set at LP horizon. Scheduling needs horizon-specific values the LP never sees. Blending needs specification and component limits. Certification needs test methods and spec limits. Horizon stays a property, not a class. P2P→O2C handoff objects — reconciled inventory, COA, nominations, ATP — connect the two domains; they are not a reason to merge them. “Produce” includes buy, exchange, throughput, and terminal blend.

06Ontology approach

Ontologies are committed for both domains. What that means practically, how it coexists with ER/Studio and Purview, and what must be decided before building.

The production pipeline — order is load-bearing

Meaning is built in this order, and the order is not optional: Controlled vocabulary → metadata standards → taxonomy → thesaurus (SKOS) → ontology → knowledge graph. Skip a step and the next one absorbs work it cannot do well — a taxonomy asked to carry definitions, an ontology asked to invent preferred terms.

Why ontology fits these two domains particularly well

Customer — the party/role problem

One organisation plays many roles, and roles start and stop over time. Relational subtyping handles this badly: you either duplicate the organisation per role or flatten roles into flags. An ontology models the role as a first-class, time-bounded relationship between a party and a context — which is what it actually is. The textbook case where representation changes the quality of the answer, not just its notation.

Constraints — the rules problem

Constraints are statements that must be checked, not just recorded. In an ontology a constraint set can be expressed so a validation engine tests a plan or dataset against it directly. The framing rule applies exactly: if a rule must be checked it is a triple; if a story must be told it is a document. Very little of this domain is story.

Six locations, one rule — plus three Store seats

Six places can plausibly hold business meaning, and the Store adds three more seats that must not be confused with them. The rule that prevents divergence is simple to state and hard to hold: every fact has exactly one authoring home, and every other location is a generated projection. The ontology authors meaning, not formulas.

The trade-off, honestly

Making the ontology the authoring home is architecturally right and organisationally harder — ER/Studio is the tool the client owns and the governance process is built around. With the ontologist and backend engineers now confirmed in house, the ontology-authoritative direction is viable, and it remains the recommendation. The fallback — author structure in ER/Studio and generate a constrained ontology from it — is no longer forced, but it should stay on the record as the option that was considered and why it was not taken.

The failure mode to guard against

An ontology nobody runs a reasoner over is documentation with unusual punctuation. Validation must be gate evidence, not a closing activity: competency questions executed against the live graph, not asserted; reasoning output shown at the review gate, not described. If the ontology never demonstrates something the ERD could not, that is a finding worth having — and it is only available if validation actually runs.

07Inherited models — approach position

Three model assets sit upstream of both domains. The method for assessing them is EPM-MOD-009; what follows is only the approach position that governs it.

The rule, published before any assessment begins

Inheritance confers no approval status. A model arriving from another project or team enters as evidence, at the source-precedence tier its own governance history earns it — and no higher. Delivery is not approval. Recency is not authority. Being ours is not conformance. Publishing this before the first model is opened is what makes a low score read as a measurement rather than a justification.

10Artifact update block

Conclusions

  • The agreed direction exceeds the Charter in six identifiable ways. All six are defensible; none should be absorbed silently.
  • Domain Modeling is no longer a non-blocking follow-on workstream. It sits on the critical path of two other programmes, inverting the Charter's sequencing assumption.
  • The two domains are different in kind — Customer is a party and identity domain, Constraints is a rules domain. Good for proving a playbook, awkward for applying one set of conventions.
  • Constraints fails the single-owner boundary rule and needs a federated ownership structure, decided before boundary work completes.
  • Constraint, Threshold, Breach, and Binding share a LimitValue pattern and are not the same object. Store KPIs reference constraints; they do not subclass them.
  • Three inherited model assets sit upstream of both domains and must be assessed and disposed before boundaries are drawn.
  • The ontology only earns its cost if it is executed — reasoned over, validated, queried. Treated as documentation it is a more expensive ERD.

Definitions added

  • Authoring home — the single location where a fact is created and changed; every other location holds a generated projection.
  • Federated domain ownership — a coordinating owner for the model's structure and lifecycle, with family stewards owning content.
  • Constraint / specification / target / threshold / breach / binding — distinct concepts with different owners, change processes, and consequences; they share a LimitValue pattern, not an identity.
  • Excel containment rule — Excel is ingestion and staging only; every Excel-sourced item carries a provenance flag and a named migration target.
  • Inheritance confers no approval status — delivery is not approval; tier is earned by governance history.

Assumptions

  • The domain content in §04 and §05 is industry pattern Tier 9, offered as workshop input, not as findings about this organisation.
  • Single refinery site for Constraints and a single tax jurisdiction for Customer unless Q-10 and Q-11 say otherwise. Either would materially expand scope.
  • The O2C and P2P reference documents remain valid as the business-architecture substitute.
  • The ontologist and backend engineers remain available for the full engagement; the capability is real but single-threaded.

11Glossary

Modeling

TermWhat it actually means
Conceptual modelThe things a business talks about and how they connect. No technology. If a business person cannot read it aloud as sentences, it is not conceptual
Logical modelThe same picture with detail — what each thing is made of, what identifies it, how many relate to how many. Still no database
PartyA person or organisation, modeled once regardless of how many roles they play. The alternative causes the same company to appear four times with four credit limits
RoleSomething a party does in a context, for a period. Preferred terms are Customer Account plus Sold-To / Bill-To / Payer — do not overload “Customer.” Ship-To is a location, not a role
HierarchyA grouping structure. There are always several and they are not the same — corporate group, credit group, and sales hierarchy answer different questions
Business keyWhat identifies something the way the business identifies it — a nomination number, not a database row number
HomonymOne word meaning two things to two groups. "Loss" in refining and "loss" in distribution are not the same number. The main reason modeling workshops exist
Competency questionA plain-language question the model must answer. Written before modeling, used afterwards as the completeness test. The single most useful technique in this approach
Definition of doneThe objective test for whether a model is finished. Without one, models stay at "nearly complete" forever
GrainThe level of detail at which something is recorded. Reporting grain and business grain differ, and a model built at reporting grain cannot answer questions below it

Ontology

TermWhat it actually means
OntologyA model of meaning written so software can reason over it, not just display it
RDFHow facts are written down: three-part statements — subject, predicate, object. "This KPI, is owned by, that manager"
OWLThe layer that lets you say what must be true — every KPI must have an owner, a tank cannot be two products at once. What makes rules checkable
TurtleThe readable text format RDF is written in. The file you actually edit
SPARQLThe query language for a graph. SQL's cousin, for triples and paths rather than tables and joins
Triple storeThe database holding triples and answering SPARQL queries
ReasonerSoftware that checks for contradictions and derives facts you did not state directly. A contradiction means either the model is wrong or the business has one
Shape validationChecking that real data meets required structure — every constraint has a limit, a unit, and an authority. The everyday half of validation
IRIThe permanent unique name for a thing in the graph. Naming policy matters early because changing it later is far worse than renaming a column

Domain and architecture

TermWhat it actually means
ConstraintA standing restriction on what is permitted or possible. Authority = physics, permit, spec, or SOL. Breaking it makes the plan infeasible or the operation non-compliant. ≠ Threshold ≠ Breach ≠ Binding
OperationalConstraintFirst-class subtype. Families (unit capacity, spec, tankage, movements, crude, regulatory, maintenance, contractual, utilities) sit under it. One of two classes; the four axes are properties
TechnologyConstraintFirst-class subtype. Control-system, historian, ISA-95, and digital-limit families (setpoints, interlocks, tag-level limits). In-or-out is a CW1 decision; if in, it is a class, not a property of Operational
SpecificationThe property range a product must meet to be that product. Outside it, you cannot sell it as that grade
TargetA desired performance level set by management. Missing it is disappointing, not illegal
ThresholdThe point at which a measured value changes status and triggers action (KPI scoring / RAG). Authority = KPI owner. References a LimitValue; does not subclass Constraint
BreachAn observed excursion past a Constraint or Threshold. An event, not a standing rule
BindingThe constraint that is active and limiting in a given plan or case. A planning fact, not a standing restriction and not a KPI threshold
LimitValueShared n-ary pattern: value, unit, quantity kind, direction, reference conditions, valid-time. Reused by Constraint and Threshold; identity is not shared
Customer AccountThe commercial object. Sold-To, Bill-To, and Payer are roles on it. Do not overload “Customer”
Sold-To / Bill-To / PayerAccount roles. Credit lives on the payer
Ship-ToA location, not a party role
Loading-Authorized PartyA party role: who may lift. Distinct from the Authorization object that records the permission
CreditAccountFirst-class credit object, typically on the payer — not an attribute of a generic Customer
Hard versus soft constraintHard cannot be violated; soft can be, at a cost. Planning systems treat one as a boundary and the other as a penalty
LP planning modelThe linear-programming model refineries use to decide what to run and make. Most constraints are already formally written down inside it — the highest-value single input to the Constraints domain
Integrity operating windowThe range within which equipment runs without accelerating damage. A safety and integrity constraint with its own governance
Authoring homeThe one place a fact is created and changed. Everywhere else holds a generated copy. The single rule that stops six tools disagreeing
ProjectionA generated, refreshed copy of something authored elsewhere. The Silver KPI-metadata table should be one
Meaning/volume boundaryOntology authors what the term means; Store + consumption semantic layer author how the certified number is computed; Gold holds published volumes. Never say “the semantic layer” unqualified — say ontology layer or consumption semantic layer
Data portfolioA grouping of data by who owns and consumes it. Answers "who supplies this" — a different question from what a domain answers
Federated ownershipOne person owns how something is expressed; several own what it says. The only workable structure for a domain spanning several functions
Source precedenceWhich source wins when two disagree. An approved artifact beats a chat conclusion, every time
