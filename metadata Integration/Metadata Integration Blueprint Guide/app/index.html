Enterprise Metadata Integration · Engineering Blueprint · EPM Program

How ER/Studio, Purview, Unity Catalog and Bigeye exchange information - and what we build to make it world-class

A prescriptive blueprint for the metadata exchange: the mental models to design by, the components to build, the information model and contracts every exchange obeys, the certification gates that make "certified" mean something, and the flows that must work end-to-end - landing in a pilot the business can touch.

v2.1 · Engineering blueprint (incorporates EPM-ARCH-META-001) Scope: EPM · Downstream Oil & Gas pilot first Systems: ER/Studio · Purview · Unity Catalog · Bigeye · Semantic layer · KPI Store

1What we are building & why

Four systems each hold part of the truth about our data. ER/Studio knows what the business means (conceptual/logical models, the ubiquitous language). Unity Catalog knows what physically exists in the lakehouse and who may touch it. Purview knows what is discoverable and governed across the enterprise. Bigeye knows what is actually happening - lineage as observed, quality as measured. Today these facts are trapped in their homes. We are building the exchange that lets them flow - deliberately, verifiably, and in both directions where it makes sense.

The result is a federated, ontology-linked metadata knowledge graph: each metadata fact stays in its operational system of authority, and we publish a gated, selective, identity-resolved projection for enterprise integration and downstream consumption. The exchange supports governed discovery, cross-platform impact analysis, data-product publication, KPI traceability, policy-filtered AI grounding, and interoperability. It does not replace the user interfaces, runtime controls, or operational functions of the source platforms.

1.1 The six qualities that make this "world-class"

1 · One fact, one homeEvery metadata attribute has exactly one system of authority. Everything else is a labelled projection. No competing truths, ever.
2 · Contracted exchangeEvery flow between systems has a versioned payload schema, compatibility rules and automated contract tests - like APIs, because they are APIs.
3 · Identity firstNothing moves until we know what it is. A central registry resolves ER/Studio model objects, UC tables, Purview GUIDs and Bigeye nodes to one canonical identity.
4 · Evidence, not vibesLineage and quality arrive as source-attributed evidence with timestamps and confidence - and only become gated through an explicit gate.
5 · Built for changeRenames, deletes, re-scans and vendor upgrades are first-class scenarios (tombstones, mapping versions), not edge cases discovered in production.
6 · Observable itselfThe metadata pipelines have SLAs, freshness-lag dashboards, dead-letter queues and alerts - we can prove the exchange is healthy.

In scope

  • Semantic-layer metric and service metadata

Out of scope (non-goals)

  • Broad production rollout without a gated pilot
  • Rebuilding reports or migrating BI assets

1.4 What the business gets

  • One portal page per data product or KPI: certified badge, owner, meaning, freshness, quality state, and "where does this number come from" - one click, no meetings.
  • Definitions written once (in ER/Studio or the KPI register) appear everywhere - Purview, UC, BI, agent answers - without copy-paste.
  • AI agents that answer with MEASURE() and cite their evidence, instead of inventing SQL.
  • When something breaks upstream, the affected KPIs show a degraded badge before the business meeting, not after.
Design posture We are not building a fifth monolith that swallows the other four. We are building a thin, excellent exchange layer: connectors, contracts, identity, validation and serving. The four systems keep doing what they are best at; the backbone makes them coherent.

2How to think about the integration

Eight mental models plus seven non-negotiable rules. Every design decision in this blueprint is one of these applied.

Core principle Keep each metadata fact in its operational system of authority. Publish a gated, selective, identity-resolved projection for enterprise integration and downstream consumption.

2.1 Metadata is data - engineer it like data

Metadata moving between systems is a data product in its own right: it has pipelines, schemas, SLAs, tests, versioning, and consumers. If you would not run a revenue pipeline without tests and monitoring, do not run a metadata pipeline without them either.

2.2 Write models and read models (CQRS thinking)

The four systems are write models - each authoritative for its own facts. The knowledge graph, portal and context API are read models - projections optimized for questions ("what supports this KPI?"). Read models are disposable and rebuildable; write models are precious. This single idea prevents the classic failure mode of trying to make the graph a new system of record.

2.3 Integrate by contract, not by convenience

"We call the Purview API and see what comes back" is how fragile integrations are born. Instead: every exchange is defined by a versioned contract - an explicit payload schema, field-level authority, compatibility guarantees, and contract tests that run in CI against recorded real payloads. Vendor APIs change; contracts let us notice before production does.

2.4 Identity before content

The same table exists as a UC object, a Purview GUID, a Bigeye node, and maybe a model entity in ER/Studio. Before any content flows, the exchange must answer: are these the same thing? That answer lives in the Identity & Mapping Registry - keyed on stable IDs, versioned with effective dates, never on names alone.

2.5 Authority is per attribute, not per system

"Purview owns business metadata" is too coarse. The correct granularity: this field, mastered there, projected elsewhere. Example: a table's technical description is mastered in UC/dbt (because the one-way scan overwrites Purview); its business glossary association is mastered in Purview; KPI identity and approval are mastered on the Store catalog (dim_kpi_metadata, status proposed | approved | drifted | archived). The registry stores this per field.

2.6 Evidence ≠ truth; location ≠ certification

Bigeye observes a lineage edge - that is evidence, timestamped and attributed, not approved truth. A table sits in a Gold schema - that is a location, not a quality certification. The exchange keeps these distinctions in the data model itself (assertionStatus, certificationStatus), so no consumer can accidentally confuse them.

2.7 Push events where you can; pull deltas where you must

Ideal: systems emit change events the moment facts change (Git webhooks, UC/dbt CI, workflow approvals). Reality: some systems only allow scheduled pulls (Purview scans, Bigeye extraction). So the backbone treats everything as an event stream internally - connectors turn polls into delta events - giving the rest of the system one uniform, idempotent, replayable mechanism.

2.8 Design for deletes and renames from day one

Creates and updates are easy. The integrations that rot are the ones that never handled: a UC table dropped but still present in Purview (the native scan does not propagate deletes), a model entity renamed in ER/Studio, a Bigeye node re-created with a new ID. Every flow in this blueprint has a tombstone and rename rule (§6, F8).

2.9 The seven non-negotiable authority rules

  1. One authoritative system is declared for every material metadata field. No field floats unowned.
  2. A projection may enrich or link source facts but must never silently overwrite them. Overwrites are explicit, rule-driven and logged.
  3. A catalog label is never the sole integration key. Use stable GUIDs, platform object IDs and canonical URIs.
  4. An observed lineage edge is not automatically signed-off KPI lineage. Validation is a separate, human-approved step.
  5. A Gold object is not certified merely because it sits in a Gold schema. Location and certification are separate fields.
  6. A widely used calculation is not automatically an approved KPI. Usage informs prioritization; only governance approves.
  7. DCAT describes access and services; it does not execute permission enforcement. Enforcement stays in the runtime platforms.

4Components to build (one by one)

Nine components plus one cross-cutting concern. Each is small, single-purpose, independently deployable and independently testable. This list can go straight into the backlog.

C1 · Connector Framework + adapters

Job: turn each system's native API into canonical metadata events. One shared framework (auth, pagination, rate-limit handling, checkpointing, retry, idempotency keys, raw-payload capture); thin adapters that only know their system's dialect.

Proud-engineer details: every adapter persists the raw vendor payload alongside the normalized event (evidence + replay); checkpoints make re-runs idempotent; a --since replay mode rebuilds any downstream state; per-asset API limits (Purview has no bulk business-metadata endpoint) are handled with batched windows and token buckets, not hope.

C2 · Canonical Event Backbone

Job: one durable, ordered-per-entity, replayable stream of metadata change events. Every event carries the envelope in §7.1. Ordering guarantees are per canonical entity, not global. Retention long enough to rebuild serving layers from scratch (or pair with snapshotting).

C3 · Identity & Mapping Registry

Job: the service that answers "what is this thing?" for every participant. Stores: canonical URI ↔ UC object ↔ Purview GUID ↔ Bigeye node ↔ ER/Studio object ↔ ontology IRI ↔ data-product ID; per-field authority; mapping method (declared/reviewed/inferred); confidence; effective dates; mapping version. Exposes resolve(system, id) and register(candidate). Inferred mappings are labelled Candidate and can never support certified KPI lineage until a steward reviews them.

C4 · Canonical Model & Mappers

Job: the one information model everything translates into (§8): DCAT 3, PROV-O, SKOS, OWL/RDFS, Dublin Core plus our EPM vocabulary. Mappers are pure functions adapterEvent → canonical RDF/JSON-LD, unit-tested with golden files. The model lives in Git; changes are versioned and reviewed like code.

C5 · Reconciliation Engine

Job: compare what systems say and decide what the exchange believes. Rules: authority enforcement (loser's value flagged, not silently kept); duplicate detection; tombstoning (UC object gone → retire Purview/graph asset with lifecycle status, since the native scan won't); lineage evidence comparison (Bigeye vs UC-native vs declared pipeline edges → Corroborated / ObservedCrossPlatform / Conflict). Outputs: canonical state changes + steward work items. Never auto-resolves a Conflict.

C6 · Validation & Policy Gates

Job: SHACL shapes encoding "what a publishable thing must have" (§10). A projection that fails a gate is blocked, the failure is routed to the steward queue with a human-readable reason, and the block itself is audit-logged. Gates are the exchange's immune system - they make "certified" mean something.

C7 · Knowledge Graph (serving)

Job: hold the gated, versioned projection and answer graph questions fast. Loaded from Git Turtle + event deltas. Treated as cattle, not pets: rebuildable from events + snapshots at any time.

C8 · Context API

Job: the single front door for all consumers - portal, BI developers, auditors, AI agents. Policy-filtered per persona: a business user gets meaning/owner/certification/freshness; an engineer also gets schema, quality rules and column lineage; an agent gets approved concepts, metric contracts and evidence - and nothing sensitive. This is what makes "the same door for every agent" real.

C9 · Write-back Orchestrator

Job: the governed reverse direction. When governance facts change (certification approved, glossary term published, remediation resolved), the orchestrator writes them back to the right system through its API: UC governance tags, Purview glossary/business metadata, ER/Studio term status. Every write-back is an explicit, logged, conflict-checked transaction with a per-attribute rule - never two connectors pointed at each other (that is how you build an infinite overwrite loop with a one-way scan in the middle).

C10 · Exchange Observability (cross-cutting)

Job: per-flow dashboards: last successful sync, lag vs SLO, event throughput, DLQ depth, gate-block counts, reconciliation conflict counts. Alerts go to the platform stand-up; weekly health report goes to governance. If the exchange is sick, we know before the business does.

6The canonical exchange flows

Nine flows define the exchange completely. If these work, everything works. Each is testable end-to-end and carries a named SLO.

F1 · New Gold product publication: UC → Purview → graph → portal (technical spine · SLO ≤ 24 h)

1
Engineering creates the Gold asset in Unity Catalog (via dbt/Terraform CI - which also emits a declared-lineage event).
2
Purview incremental scan makes the technical metadata discoverable and ingests UC tags as properties. uc-adapter independently emits PhysicalAssetChanged for deletes, external-location lineage, and notebook names the scan misses.
3
Steward assigns/validates glossary concepts, classification, ownership, contract, quality profile and publish state in Purview.
4
Data-product owner approves certification after the required quality and governance checks.
5
purview-adapter extracts the eligible record (publication state permitting) as a canonical event.
6
Registry resolves identity: UC object ↔ Purview GUID ↔ domain IRIs ↔ product ID; unknown → Candidate mapping + steward item.
7
bigeye-adapter enriches source-to-product lineage and quality evidence (§5.4 shape).
8
Semantic adapter links the relevant semantic service/metrics, if any.
9
SHACL gate validates completeness (§10.3) - block + steward notification on failure.
10
Backbone publishes the DCAT/PROV-O/ontology-linked projection; portal, graph API and policy-filtered agent index update.
11
Audit event records source versions, mapping version, approver and publication timestamp.

F2 · Meaning released: ER/Studio → ontology → Purview glossary → asset links (meaning spine · SLO ≤ 24 h)

1
Model release in ER/Studio triggers extraction (REST) with source_model_version.
2
Ontology pipeline formalizes entities/terms into Turtle in Git (human-reviewed PR); SKOS terms generated with IRIs.
3
Write-back orchestrator pushes glossary terms to Purview via Atlas API (idempotent upsert by IRI).
4
Stewards link terms to assets in Purview; links flow to graph; portal shows plain-language meaning on every linked asset.

F3 · Approval: Store catalog approved → semantic layer → MEASURE() → Gold publish → portal (governance loop · SLO ≤ 1 business day)

1
Steward sets Store catalog status to approved on dim_kpi_metadata (identity, owner, thresholds, formula pointer to the Metric View). Status is proposed | approved | drifted | archived. Do not write approval to Purview as the Store door.
2
Orchestrator projects the approved Store row into Purview as enterprise-catalog metadata, bound to the Named KPI by ontology IRI. 1:1 is one individual to one Store catalog row. Not one MEASURE() version. Purview is not the Store.
3
Gold publish writes the published values at the agreed grains. Gold publish is not re-approve. Do not set certified=true as Store status.
4
Gate re-checks the chain (approved Store row → semantic layer → MEASURE() → Gold published values → lineage evidence). Pass does not mint a Certified Store class.
5
Portal badge, agent context, BI discovery all light up from the same Store approval and Gold publish. One approval, everywhere consistent.

F4 · Lineage validation: Bigeye + UC + declared edges → signed-off KPI lineage (evidence spine · SLO ≤ 24 h)

1
Three evidence classes arrive: Bigeye observed edges, UC native lineage, declared pipeline edges from CI.
2
Reconciliation engine compares: all agree → Corroborated; Bigeye-only → ObservedCrossPlatform; declared vs Bigeye disagreement → Conflict + steward/technical-owner review (never auto-resolved).
3
KPI owner + data steward approve the traceability chain → status SignedOffForKPI; only now may the KPI publish as approved with lineage evidence.

F5 · Quality incident: Bigeye → degraded badge → owner decision (trust loop · SLO ≤ 15 min for certified Gold)

1
Bigeye detects a freshness / volume / schema / quality incident and links it to the observed asset and lineage path.
2
Canonical graph receives the incident reference and traverses impact: asset → Gold product → semantic measure → KPI → reports and agents.
3
Consumer views flip the quality state - Healthy | Warning | Degraded | Unavailable - with the incident reference; affected owners are notified.
4
KPI owner decides whether to suppress, annotate, restate or continue KPI publication - the decision lands on the KPI's audit trail.

F6 · Source change impact: schema change → affected KPIs (change management · SLO ≤ 1 day)

A source change in RightAngle or OPIS alters Bronze/Silver schemas → UC and/or Bigeye observe it → Purview refreshes → the backbone identifies the affected canonical asset → graph traversal finds Silver product → Gold product → semantic metric → KPI → reports/agents → impact events are created for technical owner, steward, metric owner and KPI owner → the changed metric is re-checked at the gate and released, or dependent products/KPIs are marked degraded. The business hears about impact from us, not from a broken dashboard.

F7 · Offline definitions: Excel to gated ingestion to candidate register (migration loop · SLO ≤ 2 business days)

Locked Excel templates (KPI sheet, glossary sheet) → JSON-Schema validation → rejects to the steward queue, clean rows upserted via API → the file is frozen as a provenance snapshot and watermarked SUPERSEDED. Tribal knowledge becomes governed candidates without copy-paste; after load, the store is the authority, not the file.

F8 · Delete / rename (the flow everyone forgets · SLO ≤ 24 h)

1
UC object dropped: uc-adapter diff detects absence → tombstone event → Purview asset retired (the scan won't do it) → graph lifecycle=Retired → portal delists. History kept.
2
Rename: stable IDs survive; the name is a versioned attribute. Mappings get a new version with effective dates; the old name remains resolvable.
3
Bigeye node re-created: identity falls back to qualified name + platform object ID; the registry re-links rather than duplicating.

F9 · Ontology / KPI definition change (meaning evolution · SLO per release)

1
Approved business change proposal → ontology/KPI Store version created with an effective date.
2
Impact analysis over mapped data products, semantic metrics, reports and agents.
3
Required semantic-model and catalog-mapping updates implemented; validation and owner approval.
4
New version published; the older version is retained and referenced as superseded - never silently overwritten.

11.2 The AI query pattern - the agent contract

User: "Why did Rack Price Capture Rate decline in Houston last month?"

  1. Agent retrieves approved KPI context from the metadata graph.
  2. Agent verifies requester access, approved semantic metric, valid variants and data freshness.
  3. Agent invokes the semantic service with the approved metric, filters and grouping dimensions.
  4. Agent retrieves policy-permitted driver/lineage/quality context.
  5. Agent returns the answer with KPI version, freshness and evidence references.

The agent must never invent a metric formula, source table, join path or report logic. Both the external/graph agent and the in-platform agent use this same door: ask meaning first, then the Store catalog for an approved row, then submit MEASURE() on the pointed Metric View. Compiled SQL from MEASURE() is the audit trail, not a second KPI formula. Agents read Gold published values, not Silver, and not the Store as a number.

11.3 Separation of discovery and access

Catalog / graph says

"This product exists; it is commercially sensitive; request access here."

Runtime platform says

"This identity can or cannot query this table/service at this moment."

  • Unity Catalog and other runtime systems enforce grants, masks, row filters and storage permissions.
  • Purview manages discoverability, classification, governance context and stewardship workflow.
  • The graph publishes policy references and access posture - never executable secrets or ACL payloads.
  • DCAT output is filtered by audience; no sensitive technical metadata is automatically public.
  • AI context APIs apply the same identity, purpose and sensitivity controls as data/service access.
  • Bigeye incidents and detailed column lineage may be restricted to engineering and control personas.

12Engineering quality bar

The bar below is what makes this something a senior backend engineer signs their name to. It applies to every component in §4.

Contract testing

  • Recorded real payloads from each vendor API replayed in CI against our mappers (golden-file tests)
  • Consumer-driven contract tests between backbone components so no deploy breaks a downstream
  • Vendor API drift detector: nightly probe against each API's schema; failure opens an issue, not an outage

Idempotency & replay

  • Every event has an idempotency key; every writer is at-least-once + dedupe; every projection rebuildable from the event log + snapshots
  • One-command replay: backbone replay --since 2026-08-01 --target graph

Resilience

  • Retries with backoff and jitter; poison messages to a DLQ with a steward/engineer triage UI
  • Rate-limit awareness per system (Purview per-GUID writes are batched; Bigeye pagination checkpointed)
  • Partial-failure isolation: Bigeye down must not stop the Purview flow; a broken gate blocks publication, not ingestion

CI/CD & environments

  • dev / test / prod for the backbone; lower environments point at non-prod Purview collections and UC dev catalogs
  • Ontology, SHACL shapes, mapping rules, contracts and adapter code all in Git with PR review - metadata-as-code end to end
  • Release trains for the backbone; connectors independently versioned

Security

  • Managed identities / service principals with least privilege per system; secrets in the platform vault; no credentials in Git
  • The write-back orchestrator is the only identity allowed to set governance tags or Purview publication state. That is not Store status. Store status stays proposed | approved | drifted | archived on dim_kpi_metadata.
  • Policy filtering enforced at the Context API, audited per consumer identity and purpose

Auditability

  • Every projection carries: source system, source version, mapping version, gate result, approver, timestamps
  • Every automated decision (conflict, tombstone, block) is logged with the rule that fired

13The business experience

"Enjoyable" is an engineering requirement here: if the experience is bad, the exchange was pointless. Three surfaces matter.

13.1 The product / KPI page (portal)

  • Header: name, plain-language definition (from ER/Studio/ontology), certified badge, owner & steward with contact actions
  • Health strip: freshness, quality state (Healthy / Warning / Degraded / Unavailable), last successful refresh - the F5 flow made visible
  • Lineage tab: a readable, left-to-right trace from source systems to Gold to the KPI, with evidence-status chips (Corroborated / Under review), not a hairball graph
  • For KPIs: the approved meaning, thresholds, review cadence, and a how-is-this-calculated link to the MEASURE() contract. Never raw SQL to a business user.
  • Access: "request access" routes to the right workflow; discovery never implies authorization

13.2 The steward workbench

One queue for everything that needs a human: Store-row approval (proposed to approved), mapping conflicts, lineage conflicts, gate blocks with plain-language reasons, migration dispositions. Every item is one decision + one click; the backbone does the mechanics. SLA badges show queue health.

13.3 The agent experience

Agents follow the contract in §11.2: resolve the concept or KPI → check the requester's policy → fetch the MEASURE() contract and freshness, then submit MEASURE() on the semantic service → answer with the number, KPI version, freshness and evidence references. When context is insufficient, the agent says so and links the steward contact - a governed "I don't know" instead of a hallucination.
