Executive Orientation

The Enterprise Performance Model is a governed architecture for connecting business intent to measurable performance and to the data, models, systems, and consumers that make performance actionable.

Business objectives→Value streams→Domains & capabilities→Processes & activities→Measurements→KPIs→Data products→Domain models→Technical assets→Reporting, analytics, automation & AI
Why it exists

Consistent performance meaning

Different reports can contain similar calculations with different logic. The EPM creates business ownership, common definitions, traceability, and reusable implementation patterns.

Conceptual umbrella

More than a KPI database

The model links objectives, processes, measurements, KPIs, data products, domain models, technical fields, and consuming decisions.

First delivery priority

KPI Store

The KPI Store is the first implementation priority, while Domain Modeling and System Integration progressively enrich and scale the broader architecture.

Current-state fact / working baselineThe Tableau reports in scope have already been migrated to Power BI. KPI definitions, ownership, targets, thresholds, lineage, and standardization were not systematically captured during that migration.
Evidence treatmentTableau XML is now parsed retrospectively to inventory historical calculations. Extracted calculations are evidence—not approved measures or KPIs. Power BI reports and semantic models are the current validation and consumption sources.
Cross-workstream baselineKPI Store and Enterprise Performance Management is first. Domain Modeling and System Integration are connected follow-on workstreams. Data Governance applies across all workstreams.
2

Project Scope and Boundaries

The detailed authority for scope, roles, lifecycle, and cadence is EPM-FOUND-001 — Project Charter and Operating Model (Version 2.0, Candidate). This index only summarizes it.

Priority 1

KPI Store and EPM

Purpose: define measurement classes, govern KPIs, inventory and rationalize report logic, establish metadata, values, thresholds, quality, lineage, and governed consumption.

Major exclusions: storing every Tableau calculation as a KPI; rebuilding all Power BI reports; treating popularity as approval; waiting for full domain models; replacing foundational data products.

Follow-on

Domain Modeling

Purpose: create a reusable, tool-agnostic playbook and reference architecture connecting business concepts, metadata, data products, models, and technical fields.

Major exclusions: broad ETL/ELT, enterprise-wide physicalization, heavy production execution, comprehensive modeling of every domain.

Follow-on

System Integration

Purpose: define responsibilities, flows, contracts, payloads, interoperability constraints, and targeted proofs of value across ER/Studio, Purview, Unity Catalog, and BigEye.

Major exclusions: broad production integration, bulk migration, production connector development, and unrelated tool evaluation.

Cross-cutting

Data Governance

Establishes policies, roles, decision rights, ownership, definitions, lineage, quality, security, compliance, data-product governance, reuse, adoption, value, and maturity.

Boundary: governance is embedded across delivery rather than treated as a separate final phase.

Current Delivery Sequence

Define the KPI Store and measurement architectureSet boundaries, taxonomy, qualification, governance, metadata, and architecture.
Inventory and classify Tableau calculationsNormalize historical logic, find duplicates, compare with Power BI, classify evidence.
Establish KPI governance and pilot KPIsAssign owners, approve definitions, set targets, implement and validate representative KPIs.
Formalize Domain ModelingReconnect KPIs to concepts, entities, attributes, data products, and technical fields.
Define and prove System Integration patternsValidate tool responsibilities, metadata propagation, payloads, and constraints.
Scale and operationalizeExpand domains, automate controls, embed cadence, and track adoption and value.

The KPI Store comes first because it addresses the immediate inconsistency in performance definitions and report logic. The Enterprise Performance Model remains the umbrella because a trusted KPI still requires business context, ownership, data products, models, technical lineage, and governed consumption.

Material unresolved conflictPrior KPI Store work contains at least two competing technical patterns: (1) a persisted tall KPI value fact with thresholds, restatement, and governed consumption; and (2) no central KPI value store, with KPIs computed from Silver star schemas as semantic views and metadata cached from Purview. Neither pattern is promoted here. Resolve through current-implementation review and an Architecture Decision Log entry.

Open issues

  • Maintainer and most artifact owners are unconfirmed.
  • Separate canonical artifacts for taxonomy, glossary, decisions, sources, and issues have not been located.
  • Extracted formulas have not been business-approved or fully reconciled with Power BI.
  • Current Power BI models, Tableau inventory outputs, and source links were not available for direct review.
  • Domain Modeling and System Integration proof-of-value domains and success criteria are not confirmed.

Assumptions and dependencies

  • Prior Downstream business architecture and data-product materials exist and can be located.
  • Power BI semantic models can provide current consumption and calculation evidence.
  • Business KPI Owners and process owners will be available for qualification and approval.
  • The first KPI Store phase can proceed without full tool integration and comprehensive domain models.
  • The Artifact Register is the planned minimum controlled set, not proof of artifact completion.

Source and Traceability Overview

Every durable statement should carry provenance and be classified by authority, currency, and validation. The detailed register belongs in EPM-SRC-001 — Source and Traceability Register.

PrecedenceSource classTreatment
1Approved canonical artifactPrimary authority; do not silently override.
2Approved decision-log entryControls material choices and supersession.
3Approved business or technical specificationAuthoritative within its approved scope.
4Current validated implementationEvidence of what currently operates; may reveal divergence from documentation.
5Candidate artifact under formal reviewStrong working baseline, not yet approved.
6Working project chatExploration and draft evidence; promote durable conclusions.
7Prior or outside-project chatBaseline evidence requiring classification, reconciliation, and migration.
8Unvalidated Tableau XML or report logicHistorical implementation evidence; never automatic KPI authority.
9General industry assumptionUse only when clearly labeled and no stronger source exists.
How specific source types are treated

Uploaded files inherit the authority of their content and status, not the upload event. Power BI models are current implementation evidence when validated. Business workshops produce candidate or approved evidence depending on formal sign-off. External research supports comparison and design rationale but does not override internal approved sources. Architecture assumptions must be visible in EPM-ISS-001. Tableau XML and local report logic remain evidence until governed.

How to Use This Index

Find authority

Use the artifact directory, then check status, version, role, and location. Candidate and chat content are not approved authority.

Find decisions

Use EPM-DEC-001 for material choices and supersession. Until created, decision cards in this index are working baselines only.

Find uncertainty

Use EPM-ISS-001 for assumptions, conflicts, dependencies, risks, evidence needed, and owners.

Find evidence

Use EPM-SRC-001 to trace chats, files, models, workshops, XML, implementations, and research to affected artifacts.

Promote chat conclusions

Route each durable conclusion to the artifact family listed in EPM-FOUND-002; record material decisions and provenance separately.

Determine what happens next

Use the priority section, status dashboard, and EPM-DEL-001 once established.
