Status: Locked 2026-09-18. Purpose: the acceptance test for the ontology. If the model cannot answer a question here, the model is incomplete — not the question. The Step 11 SPARQL regression in regression/ tests them.

Sources merged: the process-map question set (Kailey, 2026-09-17) and the decision owner's domain question set A–F (2026-09-18), minus the constraints section (parked 2026-09-18 — constraints are not being modeled). I. Process structure & navigation#

  1. What are the child processes of X, and what is the full root-to-leaf path of process P?
  2. Which processes are orphans — no parent, or no children where children are expected?

II. Responsibility (RACI)#

  1. Who is Accountable / Responsible / Consulted / Informed for process P?
  2. Which processes have no accountable owner?
  3. Which roles participate in process P?

III. Systems & data products#

  1. Which systems (XPIMS, DPO, RightAngle, …) support process P, and which processes depend on system S?
  2. What data products does process P produce or consume? Which data products have no owning process? (cf. Loss Control)
  3. Which processes have empty produces / consumes?

IV. Lanes, capabilities, value streams#

  1. Which processes sit in each office lane, and which lanes are thin?
  2. Which processes realize capability C or participate in value stream V?

V. APQC alignment#

  1. Which local processes reference APQC element E, and which APQC elements have no local counterpart?
  2. Where did we deliberately diverge from APQC, and why? (boundary notes)

VI. Shared meaning — what kind of thing is this?#

  1. What is a concept vs a named measure vs a report?
  2. Which concepts are allowed to become a number, and which must never become one?
  3. If two teams use the same English word, how do we tell whether they mean the same thing?
  4. What is the official name, what are the aliases, and what is explicitly out?
  5. Who owns the meaning of a concept, and who only stewards the data?
  6. When a definition changes, what stays the same (the identity) and what gets a new version?
  • Done when: "Customer" cannot be classified as a KPI, and "3-2-1 Crack Spread" can.

VII. Customer / Party — first domain module#

  1. What is a Party, and how is it different from a Customer Account?
  2. Which roles can the same Party hold at the same time (Sold-to, Bill-to, Payer, …)?
  3. Are those roles permanent types, or time-bounded holds? Is Ship-to a role or a location?
  4. What is a Loading-Authorized Party, and how is that different from an Authorization object?
  5. Where does credit sit — on the Party, the account, or the Payer?
  6. Can the same organisation be a customer in one deal and a supplier / exchange counterparty in another? How is that modeled without a second "Customer" class?
  7. Which concept should a customer-grained KPI bind to — Party, Account, Sold-to, Payer, or site — and why not the bare word "Customer"?
  8. What questions about "who is the customer on this order?" must resolve to one role, not a blob?
  • Done when: five real multi-role organisations can be described both ways (Party→roles and role→Party) without calling Customer a role. 2026-10-21: the party module answers questions 19 to 26 from its definitions, and its tests describe five invented multi-role organisations both ways. Customer records stay out of the ontology (EPM-DEC-001-0005), and the decision owner's customer data is not in the repository (EPM-DEC-001-0067). The decision owner chose MPC and MPLX as the real organisations; the module describes both ways their public roles from MPLX's FY2025 Form 10-K (EPM-DEC-001-0071).

VIII. Named KPI — the bind to the Store#

The ontology answers what a Named KPI is. It does not calculate it.

  1. What is a Named KPI, as a concept, before anyone computes it?
  2. What population is in the Named KPI, and what is out (in business words)?
  3. What grain is the thing we named (day × series × tenor vs a monthly roll-up)?
  4. Is a monthly average a second Named KPI, or a way of asking for the same one?
  5. When are two published series the same Named KPI vs two different ones (region, ratio, ex-RIN vs inc-RIN, blended vs regional)?
  6. What is 3-2-1 Crack Spread not (realized refinery margin, capture, $MM R&M, a homemade 3-2-1 from product and crude legs)?
  7. Which process, decision, or capability does this Named KPI inform? What decisions does it support?
  8. Which domain concepts does it quantify (region, product slate, crude marker) — without putting the formula in the ontology?
  9. How does a catalog row point at this Named KPI, and what must already exist before that pointer is filled?
  10. If an assistant asks for "the crack," which Named KPI is the official one, and which nearby series are related but not it?
  • Done when: Crack Spread has a published identity the catalog can bind, and "Customer" still has none.
  • (Editorial: Q33 merges the former section-D question "What decisions does this Named KPI support?")

IX. Process placement — where the work sits#

  1. What process (or capability) does a concept or Named KPI sit on?
  2. Is that placement certification, or only context? (If two processes use the same name, are they the same meaning — or the same label on different meanings?)
  3. What breaks upstream or downstream if the meaning changes?

X. Publication and agents — so the graph is usable#

  1. Where is the official wording published for people (catalog / glossary), and what is only a copy?
  2. What may a graph / meaning agent answer without reading a table or inventing a formula?
  3. What must that agent refuse (calculate a crack from legs, treat a report column as the KPI, answer "who is the customer" without a role)?
  4. How does in-platform assistance (Genie) get the same definition without becoming a second ontology?
  5. If two sources disagree, which definition is authoritative, and how is that marked?
