# MPC and MPLX evidence: movement losses and gains, and renewable feedstock traceability

**Status:** Evidence for EPM-DEC-001-0048 · **Retrieved:** 2026-10-19

Hamid ruled that MPC's public information takes precedence over any other source for these two gaps. This file holds what MPC and MPLX say themselves. The regulators' record rules and the industry standards, which rank below it, are in `../downstream-peers/loss-gain-and-feedstock-regulatory-evidence.md`.

## Movement losses and gains

| ID | Source | What it says (quoted) | Supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| LG-01 | MPLX LP Form 10-K, FY2025 (MPLX-S01), Note 2, Summary of Principal Accounting Policies, "Imbalances" | "Within our pipelines and storage assets, we experience volume gains and losses due to pressure and temperature changes, evaporation and variances in meter readings and other measurement methods. Until settled, positive imbalances are recorded as other current assets and negative imbalances are recorded as accounts payable. Positive and negative imbalances are settled in cash, settled by physical delivery of volumes from a different source, or tracked and settled in the future." | Movements produce gains and losses that are measured, recorded as imbalances and settled three ways: the work of CM 1.1.3.7.13 |
| LG-02 | MPC Form 10-K, FY2025 (MPC-S01), Item 1, operating facts (`mpc-fy2025-10k-fact-sheet.md`) | "The transportation links that connect our refineries allow the movement of intermediate products between refineries to optimize operations, produce higher margin products and efficiently utilize our processing capacity." | Inter-refinery transfers are movements, so the step covers them with every other movement |

## Renewable feedstock traceability

| ID | Source | What it says (quoted) | Supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| FT-01 | MPC, CDP Climate Change 2023 response (MPC-S22), p. 52 | "To meet this lower carbon intensity, it is imperative to meet standards for acquired feedstock. For example, distillers corn oil and sorghum oil feedstock suppliers are required to adhere to a supplier feedstock information compliance commitment, and include, but are not limited to, renewable biomass certifications, source locations, etc. These requirements pertain to the material acquisition & pre-processing stage." | MPC keeps supplier evidence of certification and source location for its renewable feedstocks: the work of CM 1.2.5.1.5 |
| FT-02 | MPC, 2022 Sustainability Report (MPC-S23), p. 32 | "We are also developing a sustainability due diligence process to assess risk within our renewable feedstock supply chain." | Feedstock supply-chain due diligence as an MPC control |
| FT-03 | MPC Form 10-K, FY2025 (MPC-S01), Item 1, Renewable Diesel | The Dickinson facility produces renewable diesel "from corn oil, soybean oil, fats and greases", and "The produced renewable diesel generates federal RINs, 45Z tax credits and LCFS credits when sold in California or similar markets"; "We own an aggregation facility in Cincinnati, Ohio and a pre-treatment facility in Beatrice, Nebraska. These facilities supply renewable agricultural feedstocks to our Dickinson and Martinez facilities." | The feedstock kinds and the credits that depend on them |
| FT-04 | MPC Form 10-K, FY2025 (MPC-S01), Item 1, regulatory matters | "We have developed a RIN integrity program to vet the RINs that we purchase, and we incur costs to audit RIN generators." | MPC's integrity controls on credits, which the feedstock evidence feeds |
