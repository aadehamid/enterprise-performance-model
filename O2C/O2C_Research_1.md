# Enterprise Architecture Reference Blueprint for Order to Cash in Downstream Oil & Gas Commercial and Marketing

This blueprint defines Order to Cash as a **physical-commercial-financial value stream**, not merely a sales-order and receivables process. In downstream hydrocarbons, the billable event is often established only after a custody-transfer measurement, terminal movement, retail sale, aviation uplift, or marine bunker delivery has occurred. Final revenue can depend on corrected quantity, density, temperature, pressure, product quality, terminal and destination, market-index timing, excise-tax status, freight responsibility, and evidence such as meter tickets, electronic proof of delivery, fuel uplift records, or bunker delivery notes. Industry solutions therefore connect commercial contracts, bulk inventory, terminal automation, transportation, tax, invoicing, settlement, and receivables rather than treating them as isolated applications. citeturn10view0turn1view0turn1view1turn2search0

The design is intentionally global and channel-inclusive. Tax rules, product standards, invoice requirements, and measurement conventions must be localized by country; the United States terminal-rack excise model and the European Union Excise Movement and Control System are used as representative regulatory patterns rather than universal rules. citeturn2search4turn3search0turn3search12

The value stream deliberately separates four concepts that are frequently conflated:

| Concept | Downstream interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Commercial commitment** | The contract or deal establishes product, location, delivery basis, volume commitment, tolerance, pricing formula, credit terms, taxes, title transfer, quality specification, and liability allocation. Physical-trade systems may publish these deal terms into executable contracts and expose the resulting commodity position. citeturn11search17turn11search5 |
| **Supply instruction** | A nomination, call-off, forecast, vendor-managed-inventory recommendation, retail replenishment proposal, aviation uplift request, marine stem nomination, or rack pickup authorization determines what should move. Quantity contracts commonly support releases or call-offs against an agreed total. citeturn11search39turn12view4 |
| **Physical execution** | Terminal and depot execution creates the authoritative loading event. Automated loading racks use preset controllers, flow meters, meter factors, valves, grounding checks, additive injection, and batch controls to execute custody-transfer loads. citeturn0search20turn6search4turn6search16 |
| **Financial settlement** | The invoice or settlement is created from validated actual quantities, contractual price rules, freight, taxes, duties, fees, rebates, and supporting evidence. Corrected quantities may differ from observed quantities because liquid volume varies with temperature and pressure. citeturn1view1turn10view1turn8search19 |

The same value stream branches differently by channel:

| Channel | Characteristic operating pattern |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Commercial and Industrial** | Delivered fuel to customer sites, often using forecast, scheduled delivery, tank telemetry, minimum-drop constraints, delivery windows, contract-specific index pricing, freight zones, and electronic proof of delivery. |
| **Wholesale / Rack** | Buyer or buyer-appointed carrier collects product at a terminal rack. Credit, allocation, tax identity, carrier, driver, vehicle, compartment, product, and terminal authorization must be valid before loading. In U.S. federal motor-fuel taxation, removal at a terminal rack is a core taxable event, with liability generally associated with the position holder and specific terminal-operator rules. citeturn2search0turn2search4 |
| **Retail station networks** | Replenishment is driven by forecasts, POS movement, tank capacity and automatic-tank-gauge data. Financial processing can include dealer settlement, payment-card clearing, POS exception handling, wet-stock reconciliation, rents, commissions, royalties, and site-level deductions. SAP’s downstream retail model explicitly integrates POS aggregation, dealer settlement, card controls, tank readings, and error monitoring. citeturn12view0turn12view1turn12view2turn12view3 |
| **Lubricants** | Packaged and bulk products require SKU, package, batch or lot, formulation, viscosity grade, performance category, licensing mark, warehouse fulfillment, promotions, rebates, returns, and potentially consignment inventory. API lubricant licensing relies on defined performance and labeling requirements tied to standards such as API 1509, SAE J300 and ASTM specifications. citeturn4search3turn4search11turn4search18 |
| **Aviation** | The execution event is an airport storage, hydrant, refueler or into-plane uplift tied to airline, airport, flight, aircraft or tail identifier, meter ticket, fuel grade, density, timestamp, and service fees. IATA standards address the commercial cycle from tender and bid through delivery and invoicing, while JIG standards govern aviation-fuel quality and handling from supply to wingtip. citeturn4search0turn4search1turn4search4 |
| **Marine** | A bunker nomination is fulfilled to a named vessel at a port or berth. Grade, sulfur limit, quantity, density, delivery time, mass or volume basis, sample information, and bunker delivery note become settlement evidence. MARPOL requires sulfur controls and supporting bunker documentation; the global sulfur limit is 0.50% m/m outside emission-control areas and 0.10% m/m within applicable sulfur-emission-control areas. citeturn4search2turn5search5turn5search8turn5search19 |

## Integrated Order to Cash Capability Map

The capability model below treats customer-facing, physical, financial, data, and control capabilities as one integrated domain. It reflects the dependencies found in downstream industry solutions, terminal measurement systems, commodity-pricing practices, tax regimes, retail settlement, and specialized aviation and marine documentation. citeturn1view0turn10view0turn3search6turn3search3turn1view1

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Channel and tender qualification<br>Configurable product and service quoting<br>Physical deal and contract capture<br>Formula-price and index-term management<br>Exchange, throughput and supply-agreement administration | Customer due diligence and license validation<br>Credit scoring, limits and collateral<br>Real-time exposure and availability checking<br>Order, delivery and rack credit release<br>Stop-supply, delinquency and concentration management | Forecast, nomination, call-off and VMI<br>Supply allocation and terminal sourcing<br>Trip, carrier and compartment planning<br>Rack authorization and loading execution<br>Custody-transfer confirmation and delivery closeout |

| **Customer Retention and Contract Management** | **Master Data Management** | **Governance** |
| Volume and service-level performance<br>Margin, rebate and incentive management<br>Customer, dealer and fleet engagement<br>Renewal, repricing and renegotiation<br>Offboarding and asset recovery | Customer, site and partner hierarchies<br>Product, grade, specification and UOM<br>Terminal, tank, meter and logistics assets<br>Pricing, tax, license and exemption reference data<br>Measurement, quality and calibration reference data | Process ownership and policy control<br>Data stewardship and effective-dated change<br>Regulatory assurance and records retention<br>Segregation of duties and cyber controls<br>Financial close, reconciliation and KPI assurance |

The nine pillars are mutually dependent. For example, a rack order cannot be safely released using only customer credit: the process may also require a valid product allocation, terminal authorization, driver and vehicle identity, tax status, customer license, product compatibility, and quantity tolerance. Likewise, a downstream invoice cannot be considered complete merely because a delivery was posted; the quantity basis, price publication, tax point, freight responsibility, and evidence chain must all be valid.

The integration architecture should treat the **physical completion event as an immutable business event**. An initial load-complete event should not be overwritten when temperature correction, density, tax, freight or price later changes. Instead, subsequent events should reference the original movement and state what changed. This preserves custody-transfer evidence, supports idempotent billing and gives auditors a complete path from meter to ledger.

The most important architectural control across all five scenarios is that **no correction should destroy the original commercial or physical evidence**. Downstream O2C must retain the planned quantity, actual observed quantity, corrected quantity, original price determination, revised price determination, original tax result, revised tax result and every approval linking them. This creates an auditable reconciliation from molecule movement to customer invoice, statutory return, receivable, cash receipt and financial close.
