Downstream Order-to-Cash Enterprise Architecture Reference Blueprint

End-to-end operational blueprint for Downstream Oil & Gas Commercial & Marketing across C&I, Wholesale/Rack, Retail, Lubricants, Aviation and Marine.

This blueprint defines Order to Cash as a physical-commercial-financial value stream, not merely a sales-order and receivables process. In downstream hydrocarbons, the billable event is often established only after a custody-transfer measurement, terminal movement, retail sale, aviation uplift, or marine bunker delivery has occurred.

Final revenue can depend on corrected quantity, density, temperature, pressure, product quality, terminal and destination, market-index timing, excise-tax status, freight responsibility, and supporting evidence such as meter tickets, electronic proof of delivery, fuel uplift records, or bunker delivery notes.

Channels covered: B2B Commercial & Industrial, Wholesale/Rack, Retail Station Networks, Lubricants, Aviation, and Marine.

Channel-Specific Operating Patterns

ChannelCharacteristic Operating Pattern
Commercial & IndustrialDelivered fuel to customer sites using forecasts, scheduled delivery, telemetry, minimum-drop constraints, delivery windows, contract-specific index pricing, freight zones and electronic proof of delivery.
Wholesale / RackBuyer or appointed carrier collects product at a terminal rack. Credit, allocation, tax identity, carrier, driver, vehicle, compartment, product and terminal authorization must be valid before loading.
Retail Station NetworksReplenishment is driven by POS movement, forecast demand, tank capacity and ATG data. Settlement may include dealer commissions, payment-card clearing, wet-stock reconciliation, rents, royalties and site-level deductions.
LubricantsPackaged and bulk products require SKU, package, formulation, viscosity grade, performance category, batch/lot, licensing mark, warehouse fulfillment, promotions, rebates and returns.
AviationAirport storage, hydrant, refueler or into-plane uplift tied to airline, airport, flight, aircraft/tail, meter ticket, fuel grade, density, timestamp and service fees.
MarineBunker nomination fulfilled to a named vessel at a port or berth. Grade, sulfur limit, quantity, density, delivery time, mass/volume basis, samples and bunker delivery note become settlement evidence.

5. Downstream Operational Exceptions & Risk Management

Critical Exception or RiskStep-by-Step O2C ResolutionRequired Controls and Evidence
Temperature, Density or Meter Variance1. Detect tolerance breach.
2. Hold the disputed invoice component.
3. Retrieve observed volume, temperature, pressure, density and meter factor.
4. Recalculate using the contractual standard condition.
5. Validate proving, calibration, alarms and tank movements.
6. Issue linked correction, debit or credit.
7. Remediate master data, meter, sensor or interface defects.		Observed and corrected quantities; correction inputs; API/local method and version; meter/prover IDs; meter factor; tank data; ticket; ePOD; tolerance and adjustment lineage.
Off-Specification, Contamination or Wrong Product1. Stop and isolate.
2. Seal and retain samples.
3. Trace all affected movements.
4. Test against the effective specification.
5. Release, reblend, downgrade, return, replace, recall or dispose.
6. Block/reverse billing and establish claims.
7. Close with customer acceptance and preventive action.		Specification version; source tank; blend/additive record; prior compartment load; flush record; sample ID/seal; test method/result; certificate; trace scope and disposition approval.
Terminal Outage, Allocation Shortfall or Emergency Reroute1. Detect physical constraint.
2. Rank commitments by entitlement, runout risk and criticality.
3. Select alternate terminal, exchange or substitute.
4. Replan trip, rack authorization and ETA.
5. Reprice and retax based on actual source.
6. Notify all parties and obtain acceptance where needed.
7. Bill actual execution and assess contractual relief.		Allocation entitlement; alternate inventory; approved substitution; tax jurisdiction; price differential; freight; exchange agreement; customer approval; revised order and route.
Invalid Tax Exemption, Excise License or Movement Authorization1. Block before movement.
2. Determine whether tax-paid movement is permissible.
3. Obtain controlled evidence.
4. Recalculate duty and commercial impact.
5. Reauthorize with corrected status.
6. Invoice, report and retain the movement reference.
7. Update expiry controls and review affected open orders.		Legal entity, tax ID, license, jurisdiction, validity dates, taxpayer role, product tax code, taxable event, exemption reason, movement-reference status and approver.
Multi-Party Settlement Mismatch1. Detect control-total imbalance.
2. Separate timing, missing, duplicate, quantity, price, fee, tax and ownership differences.
3. Reconcile to physical evidence.
4. Suspend only the affected component.
5. Correct at source and replay or reverse properly.
6. Clear AR, cash, tax and partner balance.
7. Obtain operational and financial closure.		Retail: site/tank/POS/ATG/card batch. Aviation: airport/aircraft/uplift. Marine: vessel/port/BDN. Exchange: partner/location/product/balance cycle. All require immutable source linkage.
Architectural control: No correction should destroy the original commercial or physical evidence. Preserve planned, observed and corrected quantities; original and revised price and tax results; and all approvals linking them.
