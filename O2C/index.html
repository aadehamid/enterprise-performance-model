00Executive Orientation

Order-to-Cash in downstream oil & gas is not a generic AR workflow. Revenue is anchored to a custody-transfer event on a fungible bulk product; the billed quantity is the output of a governed measurement and volume-correction chain (TOV → GOV → GSV → NSV) rather than a counted unit; the price is usually derived from a published index (OPIS, Platts, Argus, DTN) with formula differentials; and the tax point is fixed by excise law — at the terminal rack in the U.S., or under duty-suspension movement control (EMCS) in the EU — not by the commercial invoice alone.[R23][R28][R30][R56]

What this v1.1 update changes
  • Replaces the generic 9-group / 46-process O2C taxonomy with the governed downstream redline: 10 Level 1 groups, 64 Level 2 processes — 36 kept, 5 renamed, 4 extended, 1 deprioritized, 18 new (§01, §02).
  • Elevates “Measure & Custody Transfer” to its own Level 1 group between Order & Fulfillment and Billing, mirroring the blueprint's standalone Measure/Title chevron — a formal governance decision of 2026-08-09 (§01.3).
  • Augments the approved baseline with a capability × channel heat map (§04.2), a canonical GOV/GSV/NSV measurement baseline (§05.1), a canonical Downstream Movement Identity (§05.3), failure-mode analysis for every integration point (§07.2), a retail-channel exception scenario (§08), and quantified governance thresholds (§09).
  • Repositions MDM as the first O2C process group and governance as a cross-cutting control plane rather than a detached support pillar (§04.3).

The document is organized as a reference blueprint: the taxonomy redline first (what changed against the generic model and why), then the full 64-process taxonomy, then the operational content — value stream, capabilities, measurement and master data, channel patterns, integration, exceptions — each section citing primary and industry sources. The final two sections provide the design principles and quantified thresholds for implementation governance, the full reference list, and traceability back to every input document.

Scope note — production use

This is a production-oriented architecture document. Companion materials prepared for personal homelab/data-engineering experimentation (synthetic data generation, local semantic-stack pilots) were reviewed for taxonomy and domain-model content but are deliberately out of scope here; every operational, regulatory, and market claim in this document is grounded in the cited external sources listed in the References.

Governance decision 2026-08-09 — a tenth Level 1 group

Measure & Custody Transfer was elevated from a candidate addition inside Order & Fulfillment to its own Level 1 process group, by explicit governance decision, to mirror this blueprint's separate Measure/Title chevron in the end-to-end value stream (§03). The group holds the two processes that convert a physical event into a billable, taxable quantity: Perform Custody Transfer & Quantity/Quality Determination and Issue Custody Transfer Documentation (Bill of Lading / Bunker Delivery Note). In downstream operations this event — not the invoice — anchors title transfer, excise liability, revenue recognition, and the evidence chain for disputes.[R23][R38]

3.1 The custody-event spine: one logical chain, six evidentiary variants

Every channel executes the same logical chain — a custody event produces a measurement, the measurement is corrected to a standard quantity, the quantity is priced and taxed, and cash follows. What changes by channel is the evidentiary document and the custody point:

# Custody-event spine — channel evidentiary variants CHANNEL CUSTODY EVENT EVIDENCE DOCUMENT QUANTITY BASIS --------------------- ------------------------------- ----------------------------- ------------------------------ B2B C&I Truck/rail delivery at site ePOD + meter/ticket Delivered NSV (corrected) Wholesale / Rack Truck loading at terminal rack Rack BOL (TAS-issued) Metered NSV @ 60°F [R54] Retail Station Daily POS / wetstock close POS close + ATG reconciliation Gallons sold vs. tank variance Lubricants Packaged/bulk shipment + CoA Delivery note + Certificate Counted units / bulk NSV Aviation Into-plane fueling at apron Fuel ticket (e-ticket) Metered litres/USG @ 15°C [R6][R9] Marine Bunker barge-to-vessel transfer Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) MFM mass / sounding volume [R38][R40] CHAIN: Custody Event → Measured Quantity (observed) → Corrected Quantity (GSV/NSV) → Index Price × Formula → Excise/Tax Determination → Invoice → Cash → Reconciliation
Architectural control principle — evidence preservation

No correction, re-rating, or restatement may destroy original evidence. Planned, observed, and corrected quantities — and every price/tax snapshot — are preserved with full lineage; corrections create new records chained to the original event ID, never overwrites. This principle is what makes custody disputes (§08) and excise audits answerable from data rather than from email.[R16][R27]

4.3 Governance as the control plane

Governance in this blueprint is implemented, not aspirational. Every governed object — customer, contract, price formula, terminal, custody document, KPI — carries an explicit governance record: owner, status (proposed/approved/deprecated), provenance, and the validation gates it must pass before release. Customer data is modeled party-centrically (organizations and persons engaging in relationships, roles, hierarchies, and identifiers), which is what allows one legal entity to appear as customer, exchange partner, carrier, and consignee without master-data duplication. Segregation-of-duties rules attach to process assignments (e.g., who may release a credit block vs. who may lift), and KPI candidates are promoted to approved KPIs only through the same governance path. These mechanisms are what the taxonomy's badges trace back to: the 2026-08-09 redline is itself a governance record.

05Measurement & Master-Data Baseline

5.1 The canonical quantity chain: TOV → GOV → GSV → NSV

The single most consequential master-data object in downstream O2C is the quantity conversion rule — it turns a physical reading into the billable, taxable quantity. The chain is standardized across the industry and implemented in quantity-conversion engines (SAP QCI and equivalent):[R56]

Table 5.1 — Volume correction chain (liquid hydrocarbons)
QuantityDefinitionCorrection applied
TOVTotal Observed VolumeRaw tank/meter reading at observed conditions, before any correction.
GOVGross Observed VolumeTOV minus free water and sediment; at observed temperature/pressure.
GSVGross Standard VolumeGOV × VCF (volume correction factor, CTPL) to standard conditions — 60°F / 15°C — per API MPMS 11.1 / ASTM D1250 / ISO 91-1; GPA TP-27 for NGL/LPG.[R56][R58]
NSVNet Standard VolumeGSV minus sediment & water (S&W) deduction — the commercial billing quantity for crude and many product trades.
MassWeight in air vs. vacuumExplicit air/vacuum flag on the ticket; marine MFM delivers mass directly (SS 648 / MPA regimes).[R40]

Billing, excise reporting (gallons at standard conditions for U.S. rack removals), and stock reconciliation must all consume the same corrected figure — which is why quantity conversion is governed master data (taxonomy process 1.8) and why the measurement event is its own process group.[R24]

5.3 The canonical Downstream Movement Identity

Design artifact — one identity for every movement

Every physical movement that can become financial carries a single canonical identity spanning its commercial, physical, fiscal, and evidentiary dimensions. Sixteen fields, one key:

  1. Movement ID (immutable)
  2. Contract / agreement ID
  3. Order / nomination ID
  4. Origin location (terminal / rack / tank)
  5. Destination (customer site / vessel / aircraft)
  6. Product + specification version
  7. Carrier / driver / compartment
  8. Observed volume, density, temperature
  9. Standard volume / mass (corrected)
  10. Meter ID + proving reference
  11. Tax point + tax role (e.g., position holder)
  12. Price formula + version + index snapshot
  13. Custody document reference (BOL / ticket / ePOD / BDN)
  14. Quality certificate reference
  15. Original-event ID (for corrections)
  16. Correction sequence number

Fields 15–16 implement the evidence-preservation principle of §03.1: corrections chain, they never overwrite.

07System Landscape & Integration Architecture

7.1 The billing and compliance spine: TAS ↔ ERP

Downstream O2C runs on a split system landscape: the Terminal Automation System (TAS — e.g., Toptech TMS7, Implico OpenTAS, DTN TABS, Yokogawa) owns the physical event — gate, bay, meter, BOL — while the ERP hydrocarbon stack (SAP HPM/QCI/TD/TSW/EXG or equivalent bulk-stock platforms) owns quantity conversion, pricing, tax, billing, and accounting.[R54][R14][R2][R55] Real-time, bidirectional integration between the two is the single highest-value interface in the landscape: the BOL that the TAS prints is the invoice the ERP builds. Vendor names are illustrative, not prescriptive; the architecture is tool-neutral. Landscape guardrails for ECC→S/4 transitions follow the vendor's published maintenance and compatibility-pack timelines.[R59]

7.3 Integration control requirements

  • Immutable ticket store — custody documents are append-only; corrections chain to the original event (§03.1, §05.3).
  • Exactly-once billing keys — one custody document bills once; re-delivery of a ticket never re-invoices.
  • Clock synchronization — terminal, ERP, and banking clocks aligned; index snapshots are timestamped against the contract's election.
  • Credit-token expiry — rack authorizations carry short-lived credit tokens; a stale token fails closed, not open.
  • Index-snapshot immutability — the index value used for a price event is stored with the invoice, version-locked to the price formula.
  • Idempotent excise determination — the tax engine's answer for a given ticket is deterministic and replayable for audit.[R23]

08Operational Exceptions & Risk Management

Exceptions are engineered as first-class flows with reason codes, tolerances, and owners — not as inbox firefighting. EX-01…EX-05 are the canonical set; EX-06 adds the retail channel gap; EX-07/EX-08 are promoted candidates.

EX-01 · Canonical

Credit hold at the rack during an active lifting window

A jobber's exposure crosses limit mid-day after an index move; TAS authorization tokens must be revoked in near-real-time or trucks keep lifting. Control: intraday volumetric exposure engine, short-lived credit tokens (INT-03), fail-closed authorization, exception queue for same-day release decisions.

EX-02 · Canonical

Rack price change mid-cycle

Posted rack prices change intraday while liftings are in progress; contract snapshot elections decide which postings apply. Control: immutable index snapshots (INT-02), formula-version governance, automated re-rating for affected tickets with full lineage.[R30]

EX-03 · Canonical

Marine MFM vs. vessel figure dispute

Barge MFM total vs. vessel soundings differ; the BDN is signed under protest. Control: MFM-mandate compliance (Singapore SS 648; Rotterdam and Antwerp-Bruges from 1 Jan 2026), independent bunker quantity surveys, note-of-protest workflow feeding Manage Quantity/Quality Claims.[R16][R40][R42]

EX-04 · Canonical

Aviation ticket mismatch and exemption-certificate gaps

Fuel tickets without matching flight references, or exempt uplifts billed with tax. Control: ticket-to-flight matching at capture (INT-09), certificate master with expiry, exception invoicing for retroactive corrections.[R6]

EX-05 · Canonical

Dyed-fuel diversion and dye-status errors

Dyed (untaxed) diesel entering taxed channels, or dye status misdetermined at the rack — an assessment-scale excise risk. Control: dye status as product-master attribute, deterministic excise engine (INT-06), terminal/position-holder reporting reconciled to Form 720-TO; refund mechanics per the new §6435 guidance where applicable.[R23][R26][R27]

EX-06 · Added — closes the retail channel gap

Retail consignment shrink and commission settlement gap

ATG wetstock vs. pump-totalizer variance on consigned sites; commission settlement is held until variance is cleared. Control: daily POS/ATG reconciliation (INT-08), shrink tolerance per site, settlement workflow keyed to variance clearance — the retail expression of book-to-physical reconciliation (process 10.5).[R34]

EX-07 · Candidate

Loss/gain beyond measurement tolerance

Terminal or in-transit gains/losses exceeding tolerance (working thresholds: ~±0.2% petrol, ~±0.15% diesel) trigger halt-and-remediate before further liftings. Control: book-to-physical reconciliation with tolerance gates (INT-15), meter proving re-verification, root-cause workflow (process 9.5).[R55][R57]

EX-08 · Candidate

Provisional-to-final price restatement

Index-linked deals invoice provisionally, then restate at final assessment; mass restatements stress billing and credit. Control: exception-invoice flow (process 6.4), re-rating batch controls, exposure re-rated at final prices.

09Design Principles, Thresholds & Caveats

9.1 Design principles for the target architecture

  1. Anchor on the hydrocarbon-capable core — bulk-stock inventory, quantity conversion, and secondary distribution are not optional modules; a generic ERP retrofit fails at measurement and excise.[R2][R55]
  2. Externalize the tax engine — excise rates, rules, and filings change faster than release cycles; determination belongs in a governed, replayable service.
  3. Treat TAS↔ERP integration as the highest-value interface — real-time and bidirectional, with the controls of §07.3.
  4. Engineer exceptions as first-class flows — reason codes, tolerances, owners, and SLAs for every EX scenario.
  5. Govern master data before go-live — product UoM conversion groups, terminal/TCN master, and tax registrations cannot be corrected cheaply after stock and history are posted.[R56]
  6. Stage the rollout — start with the wholesale/rack pilot (densest capability load, clearest custody evidence), then extend to C&I, retail, and the specialty channels.

9.2 Quantified governance thresholds

Automation guardrails — measurable tripwires
  • Loss/gain tolerance: halt-and-remediate above ~±0.2% (petrol) / ~±0.15% (diesel) book-to-physical variance (EX-07).
  • Tax determination: freeze billing automation if excise-determination errors exceed audit thresholds; replayable determination is a release gate.[R23]
  • Credit at the rack: do not automate rack authorization unless credit-block latency is near-real-time (INT-03).
  • Candidate KPIs under governance: DSO; billing cycle time / OTIF on NSV; dispute-to-cash leakage rate; unbilled lift age; custody-transfer quantity variance (GOV vs. GSV/NSV); custody dispute rate per lifting — promoted to approved KPIs only through the governance record path (§04.3).

9.3 Regulatory and market caveats to carry forward

  • Marine MFM mandates spread: Singapore has required mass-flow metering for bunker deliveries since 2017 (SS 648:2024 current; e-BDN default under SS 709); Rotterdam and Antwerp-Bruges mandates take effect 1 January 2026 — bunker O2C designs must be MFM-native in those ports.[R40][R41]
  • U.S. excise rates and rails: federal rates per IRS Publication 510 (e.g., 18.4¢/gal gasoline, 24.4¢/gal diesel, 0.1¢/gal LUST), position-holder liability at the rack, ExSTARS/720-TO reporting, and the new §6435 dyed-fuel refund method announced in 2026 guidance.[R24][R26][R27]
  • EU excise movements: duty-suspension movements run under EMCS (e-AD/ARC) per Directive (EU) 2020/262; Energy Taxation Directive revision remains in legislative progress — monitor for rate and scope changes.[R28][R29]
  • Index selection is a commercial decision with compliance exposure: Argus/Platts assessments are spot-market references while OPIS/DTN rack reports are wholesale rack references — mixing them in a formula is a pricing error, and algorithmic pricing posture is now an antitrust enforcement focus for the sector.[R30][R37]
  • Retail franchise and pricing-law exposure: PMPA franchise protections for branded dealers and emerging state pricing statutes (e.g., California AB 325) constrain retail price-setting mechanics.[R35][R36]
  • Environmental credits enter O2C: RIN generation/reporting and §45Z-type clean-fuel credits are now billing-adjacent master data, not a separate sustainability silo.[R24]
