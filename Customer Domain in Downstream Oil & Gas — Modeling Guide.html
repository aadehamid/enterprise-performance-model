Customer Domain in Downstream Oil & Gas

A modeling guide for a domain that looks simple until you check a second system. This report explains why "Customer" fractures across Downstream O&G platforms, names the systems where it fractures, and lays out the Party‑plus‑Role pattern the team can model against.

01

Executive summary

In Downstream Oil & Gas, "Customer" is not one thing. It is a label that six or more systems apply to different combinations of legal entities, accounts, roles, and locations — and those systems disagree with each other on purpose, because they are answering different questions.

  • The domain is broader than Order-to-Cash. O2C is the best-evidenced, most mature slice, but segmentation, loyalty, credit, compliance, and service all touch Customer data before or after an order exists.
  • The fix is not one master table. It is modeling Party once (a person or organization, regardless of role) and attaching Roles — Sold-To, Bill-To, Payer, Loading-Authorized Party, Dealer, Consortium Member — as time-bounded relationships, not permanent labels or duplicate records.
  • Every system already does a version of this. SAP's downstream retail modules bill dealers and fleet-card companies as roles on one business-partner record; the project's own draft ontology approach reaches the identical conclusion independently.
  • Six terms need to be frozen before any taxonomy work starts: Customer Account, Sold-To, Bill-To, Payer, Ship-To (a location, not a role), and Loading-Authorized Party. A controlled-vocabulary draft already exists for these in the project repository.
What this report is, and is not

This is a synthesis for team alignment, not a canonical artifact. It draws on (a) the project's own draft domain-modeling document, (b) Downstream O&G company disclosures and O&G-specific SAP/PIDX documentation gathered and validated in this workstream, and (c) a parallel AI-assisted research pass the user supplied. 02

Why "Customer" breaks in Downstream, specifically

In most industries, "customer" is a stable idea — the party who buys, once modeled, stays modeled. In Downstream it is not, for three reasons that compound rather than add:

1. The same legal entity plays many roles at once

A logistics firm can simultaneously be a wholesale customer, a haulage contractor, a rack carrier, and a lubricants distributor. Model it as four separate customer records and you lose the shared credit exposure across all four. Model it as one record and you lose that the roles have different owners, terms, and processes. Neither a single flat record nor four duplicate records is correct — the roles have to be modeled as relationships that can be added, expired, and queried independently.

2. Who "the customer" is changes by channel

In aviation, the paying party can be a fuel consortium or an into‑plane agent rather than the airline actually consuming the fuel. In marine bunkering, the buyer, the vessel operator, and the charterer can be three different parties, and the vessel itself is often more stable and more identifying than whichever party is contracting for a given voyage. In retail, the dealer is the commercial customer and the motorist is not — until a loyalty or fuel-card program makes the motorist a tracked party too, at a completely different grain.

Real-world confirmation for wholesale/rack, retail, lubricants, aviation, and marine channel splits: Valero 2025 Form 10-K; Phillips 66 2025 Form 10-K; Shell plc 2024 Form 20-F; Shell Oman Marketing Annual Report 2024.

3. Regulatory status changes the economics, not just the paperwork

Excise registration, duty-suspension eligibility, and tax classification determine the price of the same physical product to different buyers. That is core model content, not a compliance footnote — which is why SAP's downstream modules carry excise and tax-exemption attributes directly on the customer/business-partner record rather than in a side compliance table.

SAP's Oil & Gas–CRM master-data exchange transfers handling type, source-of-petroleum/excise-tax rate keys, and mode-of-transport-for-US-excise-exemption data as part of the customer record synchronization (SAP Oil & Gas ↔ CRM customer exchange). Excise-exemption licence checks are enforced against the customer master in delivery processing (SAP IS-Oil excise duty licence defaulting). Real-world KYC/AML onboarding of commercial and trading counterparties: Shell Trading & Supply counterparty due-diligence posting.

03

The modeling answer: Party once, Role many times

The recurring pattern — across the project's own draft analysis, external O&G practice, and how SAP's downstream modules are actually built — is the same: model the Party once, and attach Roles as relationships that have a start date, an end date, and their own owner, terms, and process. A role is not a subtype of the party and it is not a flag on the party record; it is a first-class thing that exists between a party and a context.

Party→ Person / Organisation→ Party Identifier→ Role (time-bounded)→ Relationship→ Account→ Hierarchy

Five decisions that shape the model

What each stage of Order-to-Cash actually needs

Order capture needs to know who may order what, on what terms, to what location. Credit check needs the exposure grouping, which is rarely the ordering entity. Pricing needs contract and rebate structure. Delivery needs authorization and ship-to. Invoicing needs the payer and tax treatment. Collections needs the dunning hierarchy. Each is a different grouping of the same underlying parties — a model that produces only one hierarchy is unusable by O2C.

04

Freeze these six terms before any taxonomy work

The project's own controlled-vocabulary draft and the domain-analysis draft independently converge on the same list. These are distinct meanings that different systems currently collapse into a single word — "customer" — and that collapse is the single biggest source of the "unbilled for this customer" type of question becoming unanswerable.

Customer Account
The commercial object. Not a party itself — the container that roles attach to.
Sold-To
Who the product is sold to; who may place the order and who may lift, in most cases.
Payer
Who settles the invoice. Credit exposure lives here — not on a generic "Customer."
Bill-To
Who receives the invoice. Can differ from who pays.
Ship-To
Where it lifts or delivers. A location, not a party role — do not model it as a role.
Loading-Authorized Party
Who may physically lift at the rack or terminal. Distinct from Sold-To, Payer, and the site itself.

A controlled vocabulary does not decide aggregates, bounded contexts, or database structure — it names the meaning so modeling can proceed without every team re-deriving what "customer" means from its own system's column names. SAP codes like AG/SP or RG/PY do not get to win by default just because they were first; neither does any single system's customer_id column.

Source: project repository, Production-Ready Ontology 01 — Controlled Vocabulary (internal, working evidence) and EPM-MOD-000 v0.5 §04, "Customer UL — freeze before taxonomy."

The practical consequence

No single system's customer table is authoritative for the whole domain. SAP's own settlement guidance says the fix at the point of settlement is to "ensure each service station location is set up as a business partner" and invoice commissions on "the Order-to-Cash or the Customer side" of the condition-contract — i.e. even SAP's own downstream products reconcile dealer, operator, and fleet-card identities onto one business-partner backbone rather than keeping them as separate customer types (SAP S/4HANA settlement for O&G downstream retail). The enterprise model should do the same thing on purpose, instead of by accident.

What's core O2C, what's broader Customer lifecycle, what's shared master data

Not every "customer" capability belongs in the O2C pilot, and not every one belongs to a future Customer bounded context either — some are genuinely shared Party/MDM concerns. A rough three-way split, grounded in the Downstream Petroleum process framework and company practice:

ClassCapability examplesRationale
A · O2C CORE Order capture and role validation (Sold-To/Ship-To), credit adjudication, lifting agreement and rack-clearance checks, pricing and rebate application, invoicing and payer resolution, collections/dunning These are the processes O2C cannot function without, and they are where the project's first pilot sits
B · BROADER CUSTOMER LIFECYCLE Segmentation and target-segment selection, lead/prospect/opportunity management, campaign and loyalty program management, customer service and complaint handling, customer retention/attrition analytics Real Downstream capabilities with dedicated process IDs in the Downstream Petroleum framework, but they precede or run alongside an order rather than inside it
C · SHARED PARTY / MDM Party identity and matching, customer/corporate hierarchy management, KYC/AML/sanctions screening, excise and tax-exemption registration, master-data governance and golden-record stewardship These concerns are consumed by O2C and by other contexts (trading, retail-network operations) alike — they should not be owned exclusively by any one bounded context

07

Why this is an ontology problem, not just a schema problem

The project's own draft approach reaches this independently: "One organisation plays many roles, and roles start and stop over time. Relational subtyping handles this badly — you either duplicate the organisation per role or flatten roles into flags. An ontology models the role as a first-class, time-bounded relationship between a party and a context, which is what it actually is."

Practically, that means the production sequence for Customer-domain meaning should follow: controlled vocabulary → metadata standards → taxonomy → thesaurus (SKOS) → ontology → knowledge graph, in that order. Skipping a step makes a later step absorb work it cannot do well — a taxonomy asked to carry definitions, or an ontology asked to invent preferred terms it was never given. Roles such as Sold-To, Payer, and Loading-Authorized Party are natural candidates for modeling as RDF/OWL relationships with validity intervals rather than as static foreign keys, which is exactly the pattern that lets a graph answer "who could this party legitimately act as, on this date" without four duplicate customer rows.

Source: project draft EPM-MOD-000 v0.5 §06 Ontology Approach (internal, Draft status).

Recommendations for the team

  1. Do not touch the O2C pilot boundary. Keep Order-to-Cash as the first proof-of-value bounded context; treat the broader Customer lifecycle and shared Party/MDM concerns (Section 6) as sequenced follow-on work, not scope creep into the pilot.
  2. Freeze the six controlled-vocabulary terms formally. Customer Account, Sold-To, Bill-To, Payer, Ship-To, and Loading-Authorized Party already have working definitions in the repository — ratify them before any taxonomy or ontology-authoring work builds on top.
  3. Adopt Party + Role as the conceptual core, with hierarchy modeled as a typed, named structure rather than a single parent-child column — expect at least three coexisting hierarchies (corporate, credit, sales/distribution).
  4. Confirm the actual system estate against the list in Section 5 before workshops. The list here is a prompt built from what the project has already touched (SAP SSR/OGSD/RFNO, PIDX, CRM/loyalty, CTRM) — validate it against what this organisation actually runs.
  5. Resolve the EPM-LOCK discrepancy (Section 8) explicitly before any artifact repeats "Customer is modeled through order-to-cash" as an approved decision. Either locate the source file or record that no such lock currently exists, via the Architecture Decision Log.
