# Stage 2 — Prose Explanation of the Turtle Ontology

*Version 2.0.0 — explains the corrected class structure and the new Downstream Oil & Gas Commercial worked example (Rack Price Capture Rate %). See the companion "Ontology Review & Improvement Plan" document for the full list of issues this version fixes, and the "Stage 1 — KPI Ontology Design Blueprint" §1a for the reasoning behind each structural change.*

## Namespace choices

- `ekpi:` (`https://ontology.enterprise.example.com/kpi-store/core#`) is the custom enterprise namespace holding every class and property we defined ourselves — it is the "schema" vocabulary of the KPI store. It remains a single monolithic namespace today; the module sub-namespaces documented in the design blueprint (`ba:`, `kpim:`, etc.) are reserved for a future physical split and are not yet in use.
- `data:` (`https://data.enterprise.example.com/kpi-store/`) is a **separate** namespace for individuals (actual KPIs, datasets, people, events). Keeping schema and data in different namespaces means the ontology (`ekpi:`) can stay stable while instance data (`data:`) is loaded, reloaded, or swapped without ever touching class/property definitions — a standard enterprise-ontology hygiene practice.
- We reused six standard vocabularies instead of inventing our own equivalents: `rdf:`/`rdfs:`/`owl:` for the core modeling language, `xsd:` for literal datatypes (dates, decimals), `dcterms:` for ontology-level metadata (title, creator, license) **and, new in v2.0.0, for module tagging** (`dcterms:isPartOf` a `skos:Collection` — see below), `skos:` for the business glossary (`BusinessTerm` is declared `rdfs:subClassOf skos:Concept`, and we use `skos:prefLabel`/`skos:altLabel`/`skos:definition` directly), `foaf:` **new in v2.0.0** to anchor `Role` as `rdfs:subClassOf foaf:Agent`, and `prov:` for lineage/provenance (`TransformationStep`, `ProvenanceEvent`, `ApprovalEvent`, and `AuditEvent` are all subclasses of `prov:Activity`, and the raw→silver→gold chain uses `prov:wasDerivedFrom`). Reusing these means any tool that already understands PROV, SKOS, or FOAF can partially understand our graph with zero custom code.

## What changed structurally, and why

v1.0.0 grouped classes under four "bucket" superclasses (`BusinessArchitectureElement`, `SemanticAsset`, `LineageNode`, `GovernanceElement`). A review — prompted by watching Casey Hart's critique of a ChatGPT-built ontology — caught that these buckets made a false logical claim: they asserted `rdfs:subClassOf` between things that don't actually share one *kind*, purely to get a convenient grouping label. A `BusinessCapability` and an `OrganizationalUnit` are both "business architecture," but neither is a narrower kind of some single "BusinessArchitectureElement" essence.

**Fix applied:** those four superclasses are gone. Instead, every class in the ontology carries `dcterms:isPartOf` pointing to one of six `skos:Collection` individuals (`ekpi:BusinessArchitectureModule`, `ekpi:KPIModelModule`, `ekpi:SemanticModelModule`, `ekpi:LineageModule`, `ekpi:ConsumptionModule`, `ekpi:GovernanceModule`). You can still ask "show me everything in the Lineage module" with a simple query — you just do it honestly, as metadata, not as taxonomy.

Three superclasses survived this review because they pass the "one genuine kind of thing" test: `ekpi:ConsumptionAsset` (everything under it really is "a surface a human consumes KPI values through"), `ekpi:PerformanceIndicator` (everything under it really is "a quantification of performance"), and `ekpi:Dataset` (everything under it really is "a dataset at some medallion layer," and this abstraction is what makes the pairwise `owl:disjointWith` between Raw/Silver/Gold meaningful).

`ekpi:Synonym` — a class that was declared in v1.0.0 but never once instantiated — was removed outright rather than fixed. Everything it would have done is already covered by `skos:altLabel` directly on `BusinessTerm` individuals, so keeping both would have been redundant modeling of the same idea.

## Why each major module exists

- **Business architecture** exists so a KPI is never modeled in isolation — every `KPI` individual connects to a `StrategicObjective`, `BusinessCapability`, and `BusinessDomain`, answering "why does this number matter to the business." New in v2.0.0: `Role` individuals now connect back to `OrganizationalUnit` via `belongsToUnit`, so "which team owns this KPI" is answerable in one hop instead of dead-ending at an unconnected `OrganizationalUnit` class.
- **KPI model** exists to formally separate three things that used to be conflated: the *indicator* (`KPI`), its *specification* (`KPIDefinition`, `KPIFormula`, `CalculationLogic`, `Dimension`, `TimePeriod`, `Grain`, `AggregationRule`), and — **new in v2.0.0** — its *observed history* (`KPIObservation`, `DimensionValue`, `ObservationStatus`). Before this version, the ontology could describe how a KPI is defined but had no class for what it actually measured on a given day for a given terminal; `KPIObservation` closes that gap.
- **Semantic model** exists as the *governed translation layer* between physical gold data and business meaning. It is not side documentation: `SemanticMeasure` is a real class sitting on the lineage path (`GoldLayerDataset —feedsSemanticModel→ SemanticModel —hasSemanticMeasure→ SemanticMeasure —realizesKPI→ KPI`), so the mapping between data and meaning is itself part of the traceable graph. New in v2.0.0: `SemanticRelationship` — declared in v1.0.0 but never given a single individual — is now instantiated twice in the worked example, connecting `RackLoadingTransactionEntity` to `TerminalEntity` and `ProductEntity`.
- **Lineage/data architecture** exists to give every KPI a physical paper trail through the medallion layers, using `prov:wasDerivedFrom` chains so tooling that already understands PROV can render this lineage automatically. New in v2.0.0: three `owl:Restriction`s (§ below) make the medallion progression a checkable logical fact instead of only a naming convention, and `DataProduct` — previously declared but untested — is now instantiated (`RackPriceSheetDataProduct`) via the new `wrapsDataset` property.
- **Consumption** exists to close the loop from governed meaning to actual human-facing surfaces (`Dashboard`, `Scorecard`, `Report`, `AnalyticalModel`), each tied to a `ConsumerUseCase` so "why does this exist" is answerable for reports too, not just KPIs. The worked example now instantiates all four consumption types, including `AnalyticalModel` (a leakage forecast model), which had zero individuals in v1.0.0.
- **Governance** exists to attach accountability (`ownedBy`, `stewardedBy`), trust state (`hasCertificationStatus`, functional so an asset has exactly one current state), and history (`Version`, `ApprovalEvent`, `AuditEvent`) to assets in every other module, rather than living in a separate, disconnected system. New in v2.0.0: `StewardshipAssignment` individuals now carry real properties (`assignmentSteward`, `assignmentStartDate`) — in v1.0.0 the class existed but its one individual had no properties attached, making it inert.

## How the KPI observation / fact layer is represented (new in v2.0.0)

`KPIObservation` is the class that finally lets the graph hold an actual measured value: `data:RackCaptureObs_Houston3_ULSD_20260731` asserts `hasObservedValue "98.4"`, `measuresKPI` → `RackPriceCaptureRateKPI`, `observedForPeriod` → `Day_20260731`, `observedForDimensionValue` → `HoustonRackTerminal3` and `ULSDDieselProduct`, `computedAgainstTarget`/`computedAgainstThreshold` → the shared `Target_99pct_Q4FY26`/`RedThreshold_97pct` individuals, and a functional `hasObservationStatus` → `ObsStatus_Watch` (because 98.4% sits between the 99% target and the 97% red threshold). Two more observations for different terminal/product/day combinations demonstrate the pattern across dimensions and time — including one, `RackCaptureObs_Beaumont1_RBOB_20260731`, that breaches its threshold (`ObsStatus_Breach`), showing the graph can represent both healthy and unhealthy performance states, not just the happy path.

`DimensionValue` is deliberately distinct from `Dimension`: `Dimension` names the axis (e.g., `TerminalDimension`), while `DimensionValue` is a specific value on that axis (e.g., `HoustonRackTerminal3`, linked back via `valueOfDimension`). This separation is what lets a single KPI definition stay generic (sliced by "Terminal") while individual observations pin down exactly which terminal.

## How the semantic model is represented

`SemanticModel`, `SemanticEntity`, `SemanticAttribute`, `SemanticMeasure`, `SemanticRelationship`, `MappingRule`, and `BusinessTerm` are all first-class OWL classes with typed object properties connecting them to both the physical layer (`feedsSemanticModel`, `mappedByRule`) and the governed KPI layer (`realizesKPI`, `mapsToBusinessTerm`). In the worked example, `data:RackPriceCaptureSemanticMeasure` literally sits between `data:RackLiftings_GoldDataset`/`data:OPISBenchmark_GoldDataset` and `data:RackPriceCaptureRateKPI` in the graph — so a query can walk from dashboard to KPI to semantic measure to gold data to raw source field in one traversal. The two `SemanticRelationship` individuals — `RackLoading_OccursAt_Terminal` and `RackLoading_For_Product` — show the previously-orphan class actually doing its job: each has `hasSourceEntity`/`hasTargetEntity` pointing at real `SemanticEntity` individuals and a `relationshipType` label.

## How medallion lineage is represented

Each medallion layer is its own subclass of the abstract `Dataset` class (`RawLayerDataset`, `SilverLayerDataset`, `GoldLayerDataset`), declared pairwise `owl:disjointWith` so a dataset can never be mistakenly typed as two layers at once. Layer-to-layer movement is expressed with the reused `prov:wasDerivedFrom` property, and each transformation is captured as a `TransformationStep` (itself a `prov:Activity`) linked via `partOfPipeline` to a `DataPipeline`. The `ProvenanceEvent` class (`data:RackLiftingsGoldLoadRun_20260731`) captures the specific pipeline *run* that produced the gold dataset, using `prov:generated`/`prov:wasGeneratedBy` — separating "the recurring pipeline" from "this specific execution of it."

New in v2.0.0, three `owl:Restriction`s turn the medallion progression into a checkable logical fact rather than only a documented convention: every `RawLayerDataset` must have `prov:wasDerivedFrom someValuesFrom SourceField`; every `SilverLayerDataset` must have `prov:wasDerivedFrom someValuesFrom RawLayerDataset`; every `GoldLayerDataset` must have `feedsSemanticModel someValuesFrom SemanticModel`. A reasoner (or a SHACL-style validator applying the same logic) can now flag a Gold dataset that skips these steps, instead of relying on a human to have followed the convention correctly.

The worked example deliberately shows two lineage chains of different depth feeding one KPI: the `RightAngleCTRM` → `RackLiftings_*` chain has full pipeline and transformation-step individuals, while the `OPISMarketDataService` → `OPISBenchmark_*` chain is simpler and has no separate pipeline individuals. This is a documented scope choice, not an oversight — not every source system needs identical lineage granularity to be governed correctly.

## How governance is modeled

Governance attaches directly to the assets it governs rather than living in a separate system: `data:RackPriceCaptureRateKPI` has `ownedBy`, `stewardedBy`, `hasStewardshipAssignment`, and `governedByPolicy` edges; its `CertifiedDefinition` (`data:RackPriceCaptureRateDefinition_v1`) has a `hasCertificationStatus` (functional — one current state, drawn from the closed `owl:oneOf` enumeration Draft/InReview/Certified/Deprecated), a `hasVersion` pointing to `data:RackCaptureDef_Version1`, and that version has an `approvedBy` edge to a specific `data:ApprovalEvent_20260715` with a timestamp and an associated approver. `CertifiedDefinition` itself is defined with `owl:equivalentClass` as "a KPIDefinition whose certification status is Certified" — so certification membership can be *inferred* by a reasoner rather than manually re-asserted every time status changes, keeping the trust state and the class membership always in sync.

New in v2.0.0, `data:CommercialPricingStewardshipAssignment` demonstrates the previously-inert `StewardshipAssignment` class actually doing work: `assignmentSteward` → `CommercialDataSteward` and `assignmentStartDate "2026-07-15"` (left open-ended, since the assignment is still active) make it a real, queryable time-bound record — distinct from, and complementary to, the denormalized `stewardedBy` shortcut that also points from the KPI directly to the same steward for cheap "who do I ask right now" lookups. The governance chain is also reused, not duplicated: `data:KPICertificationPolicy` → `KPINamingStandard` → `KPICodeUniquenessControl` is the same enterprise-wide policy chain other KPIs would reference, demonstrating governance reuse across a growing KPI catalog rather than re-deriving policy per KPI.

Finally, `Role` (and therefore `BusinessOwner`/`DataSteward`) is now `rdfs:subClassOf foaf:Agent`, giving ownership and stewardship a recognized upper-ontology anchor — useful if this graph is later linked to other FOAF-aware systems (e.g., an enterprise identity/HR graph) without redesigning the governance module.
